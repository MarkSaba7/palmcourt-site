Questions & answers

Short answers to the questions players ask most. For setup help, see How to play; for tactics and deeper explanations, see the guides. Answers by the Palm Court team.

Playing

Is Palm Court free?

Yes. The browser version is free, with no account and no download. Advertising pays for it: the website shows ads from Google, and the game may show a short ad between some matches, never during a point. There are no purchases in the game: Fuzz, unlocks and cosmetics are all earned by playing. The Steam edition has no ads.

What do I need to play?

A computer with a current browser (Chrome, Edge, Firefox or Safari) that has WebGL switched on, because the game draws its 3D court with it. Chrome and Edge work best. For motion controls, add a smartphone or a webcam. Without either, play with the mouse, the keyboard or a game controller.

Can I play on a phone or tablet?

The game is made for a computer screen, with the phone as your racket. It runs on tablets and phones with touch controls (tap to swing), but a bigger screen is much more fun.

Where do I start a match?

Open the Play tab and choose Training, then Quick Match vs CPU. Its Match setup lets you pick how you swing, the opponent, the court, the length and the CPU level. The first time you open the game, a guided rally and a Play now button get you swinging in a minute: see Play your first match in two minutes.

Why does my player run by itself?

Palm Court is about the swing. Your player runs to the ball for you, so you choose when to swing, which way to aim, how much spin to add and how hard to hit. That is why a phone, a hand or a mouse can all play the same tennis.

How long does a match take?

Roughly three minutes for a tiebreak, eight for a short set and thirteen for a full set, going by the game’s own play-testing. You pick the length in Match setup. Tennis scoring explained shows how each format works.

Is there a tutorial?

Yes. The first time you open the game you get a short guided rally: a forehand, a backhand and a serve. You can repeat it any time under Play, Training, Tutorial, and skip it with Esc.

Can two people play on one screen?

Yes. Couch Play (under Play, Private Match) gives each person a side of the keyboard, a controller or another way to swing, and there is a doubles mode against two computer players. See couch play and doubles.

Can I play left-handed?

Yes. Set Plays to Left-handed in Settings, Gameplay. A swing to the left is a shot to the left of the screen for everyone, so swinging across your body is cross-court for either hand.

Controls and setup

Which control is best?

The phone racket feels most like real tennis and has the least lag. The webcam modes need no equipment at all, just good light: Hand cam follows your racket hand, and Body cam follows your whole racket arm. Prefer buttons? A controller needs no camera, no phone and no room to swing. The mouse is the easiest to start with.

Can I play with a controller?

Yes. Palm Court works with PlayStation (DualSense, DualShock 4), Xbox and Switch Pro controllers, and any other pad your browser reports in the standard layout, in Chrome, Edge, Firefox and Safari. Plug it in or pair it over Bluetooth, then press a button so the browser notices it. Hold a shot button, let go as the ball arrives, and aim with the left stick. See the button map and the controller guide.

How do I aim?

With timing. Swing early to go cross-court, late to go down the line. The shape of your swing adds topspin or slice, and its speed sets the pace. See How shots work.

Can I change the keys or the controller buttons?

Yes. Settings, Controls lists every key and every match button with a Change button beside each, plus separate keys for each person in couch play. A key that is already used swaps with the action that has it, and the menus keep their own buttons so you cannot lock yourself out. The controls reference has the full map.

Why are my camera swings late or missed?

Almost always light, distance or a slow picture. Put a light in front of you, stand about two metres back, and open the Camera check in Settings, Controls: it reads your picture, tells you what to fix and sets a timing offset. Camera troubleshooting goes through every case.

Why is the game slow or jerky?

First check that your browser is using your graphics card (Chrome and Edge: Settings, System, “Use graphics acceleration when available”). Then close other tabs and lower Graphics, Quality in Settings. Press F in a match to see your frame rate. The performance guide explains the presets and Auto quality.

Is there sound and music?

Yes, and none of it is downloaded: every racket hit, bounce, crowd and menu tune is made by code in your browser as you play. Settings, Audio has separate levels for the master volume, effects, crowd, music and the umpire’s voice. Browsers only allow sound after you click or press a key, so the first click wakes it.

Does the game have accessibility settings?

Yes: text size, high contrast, larger match text, reduced motion, colour-blind-safe colours, captions and spoken score calls, all under Settings, Accessibility. See accessibility and comfort.

Online play

Can I play against a friend?

Yes. Choose Play, Casual, Play a friend, then Create a match link. Send the link or the five-character code to your friend. When they join, press Start match; your surface and length are used. No account is needed. A group of up to four can also meet in a party under Private Match.

Can I play against strangers?

Yes. Quick Play pairs you with someone near your level for a short set and never changes a rating, and Ranked, Singles is the rated ladder. Both need the online backup switched on (Settings, Gameplay) and a display name. See the online play guide.

What is ranked play?

Five placement games, then a rank in one of eight tiers from Ball Kid to Legend. Each eight-week Ranked Season pays Fuzz and cosmetic prizes, never an advantage, for the highest tier you reach if you finish at least ten ranked games. Ranked play in plain words has the details.

Is online play fair?

Each player’s own swings are judged on their own computer, so timing never waits on the network, and online matches ignore your gear by default so nobody’s grind decides a match. The game is peer to peer and has no game server, so it cannot stop two players who agree to cheat; the limits, early-leave cooldowns and cosmetic-only rewards are the answer to that.

The match will not connect. What now?

Reload both pages and make a fresh link, switch one side from mobile data to Wi-Fi (or the reverse), and close any VPN. Strict networks need a relay, which is slower and best-effort. The online guide has a full list.

Privacy and safety

Does the webcam record me?

No. Each camera frame is analysed on your device and thrown away. Nothing from the camera is uploaded, recorded or stored. Details are in the privacy policy.

Do I need an account?

No. Your progress is saved in your browser, and by default also backed up online under a random player ID that has no name or email. An email account is optional: it only helps if you want the same progress on several devices.

What does the game store about me?

Settings and progress in your browser; an anonymous online backup (which you can switch off in Settings); your best scores and a leaderboard name if you choose one; an email address only if you make an account; and, unless you switch it off in Settings, short anonymous error reports. The privacy policy lists every item.

Why are there ads, and what do they know about me?

Ads pay for the free web version. Google serves them and uses cookies to choose and measure them; in the European Economic Area, the United Kingdom and Switzerland it asks for your consent first. Your game progress, player ID, leaderboard name and camera picture are never sent to advertisers. You can switch off personalised ads in Google Ads Settings. The privacy policy and the cookie policy explain the rest.

Can I delete my data?

Yes. Clearing the site’s data in your browser deletes the copy on your device. If you made an account, Delete account in Settings, Account & data removes your email, your online save and your leaderboard entries. You can also ask by email: see the contact page.

Can my child play?

Palm Court is not directed at children under 13. The privacy policy asks that children under 13 do not set a leaderboard name or play online. The ads are served by Google and are not set up for children; personalised ads are not meant for anyone under the age of digital consent where they live, so a young player should choose non-personalised ads or none.

Is it safe to swing my phone?

Hold on tight, use a wrist strap or a grippy case, and clear some space around you. Swing within your reach, away from people, screens and lamps.

Can other players see my address?

An online match or a phone link is a direct connection between two devices, so the other device can see your IP address, which can reveal an approximate location. Only play friends you trust. In a Quick Play or ranked match your opponent sees your display name and rank, never your account number, but the direct connection still shows your IP address.

Progress and saving

Where is my progress saved?

In your browser, on this device, and (unless you switch it off) in an anonymous online backup. Another browser or device starts fresh until you move your save, a private window forgets everything when it closes, and clearing the site’s data deletes the copy on the device.

How do I move my progress to a new device?

Three ways, all in Settings, Account & data: a one-time transfer code from the old device (valid for 15 minutes), an optional email account (no password, just a six-digit code), or an exported save file that works offline. The save guide walks through each.

My progress disappeared. What happened?

Usually you are in a different browser, a different profile, a private window or a different web address, or the browser’s site data was cleared. If the online backup was on, a transfer code or an account sign-in can restore it. The save guide lists the checks.

What is Fuzz?

Fuzz is the in-game currency, named after the felt on a tennis ball. You earn it by playing matches, daily challenges and achievements, and spend it in the Store on gear, outfits, racket paint and other things. It cannot be bought.

How do levels work?

XP raises your level, up to level 50. Level 2 needs 600 XP and each level asks a little more, so the early levels come quickly and the late ones take longer. The economy guide has the numbers.

How do I unlock the other pros and courts?

By levelling up, or with Fuzz for the pros. That decides which pros you can play as; all five are open as opponents from your first match. Clay unlocks at level 3, golden hour at 4, grass at 6, the Beach Court at 7, night at 8 and the Indoor Arena at 11. Raul Navidal comes at level 5, Rolf Federli at 10, Gianni Sinnari at 15 and Carlito Alcarraza at 20, or earlier for 1,000 to 4,000 Fuzz.

Does gear give an unfair advantage?

Gear changes your stats by small amounts, and every piece trades a strength for a weakness, so there is no single best item. It counts in your matches against the CPU. Online matches ignore it unless you switch on “Gear stats online”, and the Daily Cup and Weekly Cup use the same kit for everybody.

What is the Daily Cup, and does it have a streak?

One match a day, the same for every player, with a small reward and a streak of days in a row. Miss a whole day and the streak starts again at one; it is the one counter in the game that can reset. Your court-day total, by contrast, never goes down. See the Daily Cup guide.

The game

Are the players real?

No. The players are fictional characters. Their names are playful parodies inspired by real-world tennis stars, and the tournaments and sponsors are fictional too. Palm Court is not affiliated with, sponsored by or endorsed by any real player, tour, tournament or brand, and uses no real person’s name, image or likeness. See the pros.

Who makes Palm Court?

An independent developer in Quebec, Canada. The About page says who, why, and how the game and these pages are made.

Is Palm Court coming to Steam?

Yes, a full release for Windows is planned. Wishlist it on Steam to hear when it launches. The browser version stays free.

Can I make videos or stream it?

Yes, streaming and making videos of Palm Court is welcome. For press or business questions, use the contact page.

I found a bug. Where do I report it?

On the contact page. Tell us your browser, the controls you use and what happened. A mistake in a guide is welcome news too.
