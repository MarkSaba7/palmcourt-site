Reference

Glossary of tennis and Palm Court terms

130 terms, from ace and deuce to Fuzz, timing offset and the Daily Cup.

Written by the Palm Court team · Updated 26 October 2026

Short definitions of the tennis words you will hear in a match and the Palm Court words you will meet in the menus, in alphabetical order. Each entry is written for this game: where a number is given, it is the number the game uses. Longer explanations live in the guides, and the entries link to them.

A · B · C · D · E · F · G · H · I · K · L · M · N · O · P · Q · R · S · T · U · V · W · X

A

Ace
A serve the receiver cannot touch. In Palm Court an ace pays 6 XP and 3 Fuzz in a match, and is worth 150 points in a Survive or Blitz round (100 for the point and 50 for the ace).
Achievement
A long-term goal that pays Fuzz and XP once, and sometimes a title. The browser game has 36. See the economy guide.
Ad court
The left-hand half of the court from the server’s point of view. The server serves from the left of the centre mark on the second, fourth and sixth points of a game, into the receiver’s left-hand box. See also deuce court.
Advantage
The point won after deuce. The scoreboard shows AD for the player who has it. Win the next point and you win the game; lose it and the score returns to deuce.
All-court player
A player who is comfortable on the baseline and at the net and chooses between them as the point demands. In Palm Court, Rolf Federli is the all-court attacker. See the pros in depth.
Alley (doubles alley)
The strip 1.37 m wide along each side of the court that is in play for doubles but not for singles. A serve must still land in the normal service box. See couch play and doubles.
Approach shot
A groundstroke hit deep as you move towards the net, so the opponent’s reply is a hard one to pass. See net play.
Auto quality
The default Graphics setting. It picks a preset from your graphics chip, checks the first calm seconds once, and lowers resolution (and sometimes the preset) when frames run slow. See the performance guide.

B

Backhand
A stroke played on the side of the body opposite the racket hand. The game labels each incoming ball Forehand or Backhand so you know which swing to make. Most pros hit it with two hands; Rolf Federli uses one.
Bagel
A set won without the opponent winning a single game (6-0 in a full set). The game counts them in your career stats.
Ball machine
The feeder in the training drills. It sends balls to alternating sides, and in the rally streak it speeds up every few good returns.
Baseline
The line at the back of each end of the court, 11.885 m from the net. A ball that touches any part of it is in. Baseliners spend most of a point behind it.
Baseliner
A player who prefers to rally from behind the baseline and wait for the opponent to err or open the court. Nolan Jokavic and Gianni Sinnari are both baseliners, in very different ways.
Blitz
An arcade round: 90 seconds against a Club-level CPU, scoring 100 points for every point you win plus bonuses. The clock starts with your first serve. See the drills guide.
Body cam
The webcam control that follows your racket arm (shoulders, elbow and wrist) with a body tracker of about 6 MB. It reads your wrist against your shoulders, so where you stand in the room does not matter. See camera troubleshooting.
Bounce spot
A mark that shows where the ball will land, to help your timing. It is the Bounce spot switch in Settings, Gameplay, on by default.
Break (of serve)
Winning a game that your opponent served. Breaks decide most sets. A break point is a point that would win the receiver such a game.

C

Camera check
The setup screen for the webcam controls: framing, sensitivity, a swing test (forehand, backhand, toss) and a timing test, with a live line of advice and a Camera timing card. Open it from Settings, Controls.
Captions
A line of commentary on the big points in practice and offline matches. Settings has Off, Captions, and Captions plus voice. See accessibility.
Celebration
How your player reacts after winning a point or the match. Celebrations are Store items and each pro has their own.
Chair umpire
The official who sits on the high chair and calls the score. In Palm Court the umpire’s voice calls it in your system’s speech voice, server’s score first, and the score is always on screen too.
Challenger
The first tier of the World Tour, and the name of a level of professional tournament. In the free browser game it is the only tour tier. It is also the third rank tier in ranked play.
Clean timing
A swing that lands close to the ideal moment, shown as “On time”. In a match each one pays 1 XP and 1 Fuzz (up to 40 a match), and in target practice it adds a point.
Click mode
A mouse setting in which a click is the swing, with a flick just before it to aim. It is the default on touch screens. The alternative is swipe.
Club (CPU level)
The middle CPU level: steady, runs at 5.1 m/s, reacts in 0.30 s, and pays 1.25 times the Rookie rewards. See CPU level.
Contact point
The place and moment the racket meets the ball. In Palm Court the moment of contact is your swing: timing at contact aims the ball.
Couch play
Two people, one screen, each with a keyboard side, a controller, or (for one person only) a phone or a webcam. Nothing from a couch match is recorded. See couch play and doubles.
Counter-puncher
A player who wins by defending, returning everything and letting the opponent make errors. The game labels Nolan Jokavic a counter-puncher because of his defence and consistency.
Court day
A day on which you finish a match, a drill or an arcade round. The total never goes down. Two, four and six court days in a week pay bonuses. See the Daily Cup guide.
CPU level
How well the computer opponent plays: Rookie, Club or Pro. A level sets running speed, reaction time, accuracy, serve pace and shot choice, and multiplies your rewards by 1, 1.25 or 1.6. See the pros in depth.
Create a player
The editor for your own figure: face, hair, skin, build and eyes. It is free and only changes looks. See create a player.
Cross-court
A shot hit diagonally to the opposite side of the court. It is the safest direction because the net is lowest in the middle and the diagonal is the longest path. Swinging early pulls the ball cross-court.

D

Daily challenge
One of three small goals a day (such as “Serve 3 aces”), from a pool of seventeen, with one free reroll. Each pays 150 to 400 XP and 100 to 250 Fuzz.
Daily Cup
One match a day, the same for every player, with the setup worked out from the date. It pays a little every day and keeps a streak of days in a row. See the Daily Cup guide.
Dead zone (stick)
How far a controller stick has to move before it counts as aiming. Raise it if your stick drifts. The setting runs from 0.10 to 0.40 and starts at 0.22.
Deep
A ball that lands close to the opponent’s baseline. Deep shots keep the opponent back and make attacking harder for them.
Deuce
A game score of 40-40. The next point gives advantage. A game can return to deuce many times. See tennis scoring explained.
Deuce court
The right-hand half of the court from the server’s point of view. The server starts each game there and serves from it on the first, third and fifth points, into the receiver’s right-hand box. See also ad court.
Double fault
Two serve faults in a row, which loses the point. In Survive a double fault costs a life.
Doubles
Two players a side on the wider court, with the alleys in play. In Palm Court you can play doubles in a Quick Match against CPU opponents and in couch play; the scoring is the same as singles.
Down the line
A shot hit straight along a sideline. It is riskier than cross-court because the court is narrower there. Swinging a touch late sends the ball down the line.
Drill
A practice exercise with a ball machine and a score: target practice, serve speed, rally streak and drop shot touch, each graded S to D. See the drills guide.
Drive (flat)
A groundstroke hit with little spin, fast and low over the net. It is the default shot of the controller’s ✕ or A button and of a plain swing.
Drop shot
A soft shot that barely clears the net and lands short, to pull the opponent forward. It has little margin: from the baseline it can go into the net, and one that lands deep sits up for the opponent to attack. Carlito Alcarraza loves them, and lobs the player who chases one. In the game: D, or L1 or LB with slice.

E

Early/late bar
The small display after each shot that shows how early or late your swing was. Switch it on or off in Settings (Timing meter).
Elite (CPU)
The extra-hard opponent that Survive reaches after you win 14 points. It is a notch above a Pro CPU and does not appear in normal matches.
Elo rating
The rating system behind ranked play: a win over a stronger player pays more than a win over a weaker one. In Palm Court you start at 1,000, and no single match moves you by more than 70. See ranked play explained.

F

Fault
A serve that does not land in the correct service box (or that hits the net and lands outside it). The first fault gives a second serve.
Forehand
The stroke played on the same side as the racket hand. It is the more natural, more powerful stroke for most players.
Frame rate (fps)
How many pictures a second the game draws (or the camera delivers). Press F in a match to see it. Under about 40 for the game, or under 20 for a webcam, is where problems begin.
Friend link
A web link or five-character code that lets a friend join your online match directly, with no account. The host’s court and length are used. See the online play guide.
Fuzz
The in-game currency, named after the felt on a tennis ball. You earn it by playing and spend it in the Store. It can never be bought with money.

G

Game
A unit inside a set: the first player to win four points, two clear, wins it. One player serves for the whole game.
Gear
Five slots (frame, strings, shoes, grip and dampener) in five tiers (Starter, Club, Pro, Elite, Legend). Each piece trades a strength against a cost. See gear and upgrades in numbers.
Golden hour
The time of day with a low, warm sun and long shadows. It unlocks at level 4 and changes only how the court looks and sounds. See venues and times of day.
Grade
The S, A, B, C or D a drill ends with, from fixed score thresholds for each drill.
Groundstroke
A forehand or backhand hit after the ball has bounced. Most of a rally is groundstrokes. See groundstroke tactics.

H

Hand cam
The webcam control that follows your racket hand with a hand tracker of about 10 MB. It needs good light in front of you and about two metres of distance. See camera setup.
Hard court
The default surface: a medium-height bounce that keeps 76 percent of the ball’s downward speed, with medium grip. The Beach Court and the Indoor Arena also play as hard courts.
Hit harder
A held modifier (Shift, or R1 or RB on a controller) that pushes a shot close to full power and aims it nearer the lines. Faster and riskier.
Hold (serve)
Winning a game on your own serve. The opposite of a break.

I

Instant replay
A short film from several angles after an ace, a winner or a close line call. Skip it with a swing or Space, or turn replays off in Settings, Gameplay.

K

Kick serve
A serve with heavy topspin that dips quickly into the box and bounces up high. On a controller it is the ○ or B button.

L

Latency (lag)
The delay between something happening and its effect on screen. A webcam adds 30 to 120 ms of its own; the game compensates with the timing offset. See camera troubleshooting.
Let
A serve that clips the net and still lands in the correct box. It is replayed without counting as a fault.
Level (player level)
Your progress number, from 1 to 50, raised by XP. Levels unlock courts, times of day, venues, pros and gear. See XP and levels in numbers.
Line review
The close-up check of a close call. The ball leaves an oval mark about 6.5 cm across and 11 cm long, and the ball is in if any part of that mark touches the line, which is the real rule.
Lob
A high, deep shot over an opponent at the net. In the game: E on the keyboard, or △ or Y on a controller. A lob is the usual answer to a player who comes in too close.
Locker
The Store’s companion tab where you equip what you own and see Your stats: your pro and gear together, as bars around 1.0.
Love
Zero in tennis scoring. “Fifteen, love” means the server has one point and the receiver none.

M

Match
The whole contest. In Palm Court it is one set: a tiebreak, a short set or a full set. See tennis scoring explained.
Match code
The five-character code for a friend link or a party, written with letters and digits that cannot be confused (no 0, O, 1, I, L, S or 5).

N

Net cord
A ball that clips the top of the net and then dribbles over or drops back. A net cord on a serve that lands in is a let.
Neutral kit
The same standard equipment for everyone, used in the Daily Cup and Weekly Cup (and in online matches unless you switch gear on), so the result reflects skill rather than gear.
Next goal
The card on the Play page that shows the closest reward you can finish, with an estimate such as “about one match”.

O

Online backup
An anonymous copy of your progress kept online under a random player ID. It is on by default and can be switched off in Settings. See saving and moving your progress.
Out
A ball that lands outside the lines. In Palm Court the word flashes on screen and the line judge calls it, and a close one gets a line review.
Overhead (smash)
A hard shot hit downward at a ball above your head, usually to finish a lob. See net play.

P

Party
An online room for up to four friends, shared by link or code. The leader picks the court and length and starts a match between two ready members; the winner stays on.
Passing shot
A shot hit past an opponent at the net, down the line or cross-court.
Persona (style numbers)
The nine numbers, from 0 to 1, that describe how a CPU player plays: aggression, topspin, slice, drop shots, net play, serve, consistency, defence and speed. See the pros in depth.
Phone racket
The control that uses your phone’s motion sensors as the handle of a racket. You pair it by scanning a QR code. See phone racket setup.
Ping
The time a message takes to reach your opponent and return, in milliseconds. The online badge is green under 90, amber under 180 and red above.
Placement games
The first five ranked games, in which your rating moves a lot and no rank is shown yet.
Point
The smallest unit of scoring: one rally, from serve until the ball is out, in the net, or unreturned. Points are called love, 15, 30 and 40 within a game.
Power meter
The small meter by your player that shows how long a controller shot has been charged. It can be hidden in Settings once you no longer need it.
Pro (the five)
One of the five fictional characters: Nolan Jokavic, Raul Navidal, Rolf Federli, Gianni Sinnari and Carlito Alcarraza. Not to be confused with the Pro CPU level. See the pros.

Q

Quick Play
The online queue that pairs you with a stranger near your level, for a short set, without changing any rating.

R

Rally
The exchange of shots in a point after the serve and return. A rally of six or more shots earns bonus XP and Fuzz in a match.
Rally streak
The drill in which you keep the ball in play for 90 seconds against a feeder that speeds up every few good returns. Your score is your longest run of shots.
Ranked Season
An eight-week period of ranked play that pays Fuzz and cosmetic prizes at the end, for the highest tier you reach, if you play at least ten ranked games. See ranked play explained.
Receiver
The player who returns the serve. The receiver’s first shot, the return, is often the most important of the point.
Reduce motion
A setting that removes camera shake, the lens punch on big hits and hit flashes, and shortens animations. It follows your system’s preference until you choose.
Relay (TURN)
A server that passes game data between two browsers that cannot connect directly. It works, but it is slower. The ping badge says “relay” when one is in use.
Rookie (CPU level)
The easiest CPU level: slower, wilder and with a lower first serve. It pays the base rewards.

S

Save file
The file you can export from Settings, Account & data and import elsewhere. It holds settings and progress and never an account or sign-in.
Season Points
Points from matches, challenges, goals and Cups that fill the monthly season’s six-step track, ending in a season title and a racket paint. Not to be confused with a Ranked Season.
Second serve
The serve you get after a fault. It is slower and safer than a first serve, and a CPU second serve tops out around 140 km/h.
Seed
In real tennis, a top player placed in the draw so the best do not meet early. In ranked play, Seed is the fifth tier, starting at a rating of 1,200.
Serve
The shot that starts a point: toss the ball, then hit it into the diagonal service box. In Palm Court you toss first, then swing as the ball drops. See serve and return.
Serve and volley
Following your serve to the net to volley the return. Rolf Federli does it on about three first serves in ten.
Service box
One of the two boxes on each side of the net into which a serve must land: 6.40 m deep and 4.115 m wide.
Set
A group of games. A full set goes to the first to six games with a two-game lead, and a tiebreak at 6-6. A short set is first to four.
Short set
Palm Court’s default length: first to four games, two clear, with a tiebreak at 4-4.
Slice
A stroke that brushes under the ball and gives it backspin, so it floats and stays low. Swipe downward, or press S or □ or X.
Split step
A small hop a player takes as the opponent hits, to be ready to move either way. Your player and the CPU do it for you.
Stick flick
The controller scheme in which you swing by flicking the right stick; the speed of the flick is the power. The other scheme is holding a shot button.
Store
Where Fuzz is spent on gear, outfits, racket paint, headwear, wristbands, celebrations and pros. Nothing in it costs real money.
Streak (Daily Cup)
The number of days in a row you have finished the Daily Cup. It resets to one if a whole day is missed, and it is the one part of the game that can.
Surface
The court material: hard, clay or grass. In Palm Court it changes the bounce and the grip on spin, and nothing else.
Survive
An arcade round with three lives, against a CPU that gets sharper with every point you win.
Swipe
A quick flick of the mouse, a touchpad or a finger that counts as a swing: its direction aims, its tilt picks topspin or slice, and its speed sets the power.

T

The T
The point where the service line meets the centre service line. A serve “to the T” goes down the middle of the court, close to the receiver’s body line.
Tiebreak
A game of plain numbers, won by the first to seven points with a two-point lead. The serve changes after the first point and then every two. In Palm Court it is also the shortest match length.
Tier (rank)
One of the eight ranked levels: Ball Kid, Club Player, Challenger, Contender, Seed, Finalist, Champion and Legend. Each except Legend has three divisions.
Time of day
The light on the court: day, golden hour or night (plus two more in the Steam edition). It changes how the court looks and the sound of the open air, never the ball.
Timing offset
A setting from minus 100 to plus 250 ms that tells the game how late your camera’s pictures arrive. It learns as you play and can be set by hand. See camera troubleshooting.
Topspin
Forward spin that makes the ball dip and kick up on landing. A low-to-high swing adds it. Raul Navidal’s topspin is the heaviest of the five pros.
Tracker
The small model that finds your hand or arm in the camera picture. It runs inside your browser, on your computer, and downloads once the first time you use a camera control.
Transfer code
A short one-use code, valid for 15 minutes, that moves your progress to another device. See saving and moving your progress.

U

Unforced error
A mistake that the opponent did not cause, such as hitting a comfortable ball into the net. Playing a safer shot on big points avoids them.

V

Venue
The place a match is set in: the classic stadium, the Beach Court or the Indoor Arena. A venue changes scenery, crowd and sound, and plays as a hard court.
Volley
A shot hit before the ball bounces, usually at the net. Short, firm and early works best. See net play.

W

Weekly Cup
A themed match that changes every Monday (UTC). Scores of 250, 500 and 700 earn Bronze, Silver and Gold, each paid once a week.
Weekly goal
One of three larger goals a week, such as “Win 6 matches”, paying 300 to 600 XP and 200 to 380 Fuzz.
Wide
A serve aimed at the outer corner of the service box, near the sideline. It pulls the receiver off the court.
Wind-up (take-back)
Taking the racket back before the forward swing. Both camera controls read it as a wind-up, not a swing, so taking the racket back first is fine.
Winner
A shot the opponent cannot reach or return, other than a serve ace. A winner pays 4 XP and 2 Fuzz in a match.
World Tour
A season of weekly events with a ranking, prize Fuzz and trophies. The free browser game plays the Challenger tier. See levels, Fuzz and the Store.

X

XP
Experience points: what a match, a drill or a goal pays towards your level. Level 2 needs 600; level 50 needs 50,150 in all.
Palm Court

Motion-controlled tennis in your browser. Swing your phone or your hand, or pick up a controller, and the player on court does the running.
