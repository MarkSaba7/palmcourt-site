Privacy policy

Palm Court needs no sign-up (an email is stored only if you choose to create an optional account), has no passwords and no analytics, and the camera picture never leaves your device. Your progress is backed up online under a random ID unless you turn that off. The game is free to play and shows no ads.

Effective date: 12 October 2026

In short

  • Camera frames are processed on your device and are never uploaded, recorded or stored.
  • Your settings and progress are saved in your own browser.
  • Your progress is backed up online under a random player ID (no name, email or camera data). If you pick a leaderboard name, that name and your best scores are public. You can turn the backup off in Settings.
  • An email address is stored only if you choose to create an account (optional). It is used only to send you sign-in codes, is kept by our database provider, Supabase, and you can delete it in the game (Settings, Profile, Account) or by asking us.
  • Online matches and the phone racket connect devices directly, so the other device can see your IP address.
  • If the game breaks it can send a short anonymous error report (no name, player ID or save data); you can switch that off in Settings (About, “Send error reports”).
  • There are no ads, no advertising cookies and no analytics. We don’t sell your personal information.
  • To see or delete your data, email palmcourt.game@gmail.com.

Who we are

Palm Court is made and run by its independent developer (“we”, “us”), who is based in Quebec, Canada, and is the person responsible for the personal information described here that Palm Court itself handles (the online backup and leaderboards). Contact: palmcourt.game@gmail.com.

Camera

If you choose the Camera control (Hand cam or Body cam), the game asks your browser for the camera. Each frame is analysed in the page, on your device (body and hand tracking use Google’s MediaPipe library running locally), and then thrown away. Frames are never sent over the network, recorded or saved, and no biometric or face data is created or kept. You can revoke camera access at any time in your browser’s site settings. The phone racket page can also use the phone’s camera, if you allow it, only to read the pairing QR code; those frames are likewise processed on the phone and never sent or saved.

Body cam diagnostics (optional, off unless you switch it on). To help tune the Body cam on your camera, Settings > Controls has a switch that keeps a log of numbers only while you use Body cam: where the model placed your shoulders, elbows, wrists and hips in each frame (as coordinates), when the frames arrived and how long the model took, which swings the game counted and why, and which balls were coming. It never contains a picture, video or sound. It stays in the page’s memory on your device and is not saved or sent anywhere; the Export button saves it as a file and the Copy button puts it on your clipboard, and only you decide whether to share it. It is gone when you close the page or press Start again.

Hand cam diagnostics (optional, off unless you switch it on). In the same way, the Camera timing card in the camera check has a Record a session button for the Hand cam. While it is on, it keeps numbers only: where the tracked point of your hand was in each camera picture (a position and a size), when the picture was taken (in milliseconds from the start of the recording, not the time of day), how late the result reached the game, the swings the game counted and whether they were early or late, and your timing offset. It never contains a picture, video or sound, and no name, account or device identifier. It stays in the page’s memory on your device and is not sent anywhere; the Download file button saves it and the Copy button puts it on your clipboard, and only you decide whether to share it. It is gone when you close the page or press Clear.

Data saved on your device

Palm Court saves your settings (display name, controls, graphics, sensitivity), your progress (level, XP, Fuzz, unlocks, match history and stats), and, if online backup is on, the sign-in token for your anonymous backup, in your browser’s storage (IndexedDB and local storage). The phone racket page saves its own preferences and the last game it paired with on the phone. This stays in that browser on that device; clearing the site’s data deletes it. The cookie policy lists each item.

Online backup and leaderboards

Palm Court backs up your progress online, so it can be recovered if the copy in your browser is lost or damaged, and it runs leaderboards. This is on by default. You can turn it off in Settings (Gameplay, “Online backup & leaderboards”); it takes effect after you reload the page.

When it is on, your browser creates an anonymous account with our database provider, Supabase. It has a random player ID: no email, password or camera data. We store under that ID:

  • a copy of your progress (the same data as above), synchronised as you play;
  • your best scores (level, World Tour ranking points, longest win streak and fastest serve), all-time and per monthly season;
  • the leaderboard name you choose, if you choose one; and
  • technical data needed to run the service securely, such as sign-in timestamps, rate-limit counters and, in the provider’s logs, your IP address and browser details.

If you choose a leaderboard name, that name and your scores are visible to every player, so please don’t use your real name or other personal details. Without an account the backup belongs to the browser it was made in; it can’t follow you to another device (a short transfer code can move it).

Optional account. If you choose “Continue with email” in Settings (Profile, Account), you enter your email address and we send you a one-time code; there is no password. We store that email address with your anonymous player ID at Supabase, so the same progress, name and leaderboard entries follow you to any device you sign in on (for example the website and the Steam version). The email is used only to send you sign-in codes and is not used for marketing or shared with anyone else; the codes are sent through our email delivery provider, which handles your address only to deliver them. Signing out removes your progress from that device (it stays in your account). “Delete account” in the same place removes your email, your online save, your leaderboard entries and your online-play data from our database. You can also ask us to delete it by email.

Error reports. If the game breaks, it can send a short anonymous report (the error, game version, browser type and screen size; no name, player ID or save data) to our database provider, Supabase, which deletes it after 30 days; you can switch this off in Settings (About, “Send error reports”).

Online play and the phone racket

To connect two browsers (a friend’s game, or your phone as a racket), the game uses the PeerJS public connection service, which sees both devices’ IP addresses to introduce them, and a STUN server run by Google that tells each device its public address. After that the devices talk directly (WebRTC). If a direct link isn’t possible, the encrypted connection may be relayed through a PeerJS relay (TURN) server. Only game data is sent: swings and phone motion readings, ball positions, scores, settings needed for the match and your display name. As with any direct connection, the other device can see your IP address, which can reveal your approximate location, so only play with people you trust.

Quick match, ranked matches and parties. These use the same online service as the backup and leaderboards (Supabase) unless you switch it off in Settings. While you search or sit in a party it stores your anonymous account number, your display name, your rating and a time stamp; for a match it also stores the match result and, until the match ends, a room code. Ratings, results and early-leave counts are kept to run the ranked ladder and to limit abuse, and old match records are deleted after about two weeks. Opponents see your display name and rank, never your account number. Deleting your account (see Retention) deletes this data too.

Advertising and cookies

Palm Court is free to play in your browser and shows no advertising, on this website or in the game. It sets no cookies and uses no advertising identifiers or analytics; the cookie policy lists the browser storage it does use. The Steam edition is a separate paid release.

Other requests your browser makes

Like any web page, Palm Court downloads its libraries and fonts from content networks (jsDelivr, Google Fonts, and Google’s storage for the hand-tracking model), and the website is delivered by its hosting provider (GitHub Pages or Cloudflare). Those services see your IP address and browser details when they deliver the files, and may keep them in server logs for security. Palm Court uses no analytics or tracking of its own.

Why we use data (legal bases)

Where the EU or UK GDPR applies, we rely on:

  • performance of our agreement with you (the terms of use) to save and back up your progress, run leaderboards and connect online matches you start;
  • our legitimate interests in keeping the service secure, preventing cheating and abuse.

Who receives data

We don’t sell your personal information for money. Data is shared only with the service providers that run Palm Court on our behalf (Supabase for the backup and leaderboards; PeerJS and Google for connecting online matches; our hosting and content networks), with the other player in an online match (your display name and IP address), with everyone (your leaderboard name and scores, if you set a name), and where the law requires it or to protect players, the service or our rights. These providers may process data in the United States and other countries; where the law requires, transfers rely on safeguards such as the European Commission’s standard contractual clauses or an adequacy decision.

How long we keep data

  • Data on your device stays until you clear the site’s data or reset it in the game.
  • Your online backup, scores and leaderboard name are kept while the online backup feature runs, unless you ask us to delete them sooner. An account’s email address is kept until you delete the account (Settings, Profile, Account) or ask us to. Monthly season scores are kept as season history. If we shut the online features down, we will delete this data.
  • Technical logs held by our providers are kept for the periods set by those providers, typically days to weeks.

Children

Palm Court is not directed at children under 13, and we don’t knowingly collect personal information from children under 13 (or under the age of digital consent where they live). Children under 13 should not set a leaderboard name or play online. If you believe a child under 13 has given us personal information, for example in a leaderboard name, contact us and we will delete it.

Your choices and rights

You can turn the online backup off in Settings, delete your account (if you made one) in Settings, Profile, Account, clear the site’s data to delete everything on your device. To get a copy of, correct or delete the data we hold in the online backup, email palmcourt.game@gmail.com with your leaderboard name (if you set one) and roughly when you last played; because the backup has no name or email, we may need these details to find it, and we may not be able to identify a backup that has no leaderboard name.

EU, EEA, UK and Swiss residents have the right to access, correct, delete, restrict or move their personal data, to object to its use, and to withdraw consent at any time. You also have the right to complain to your data protection authority (in the UK, the Information Commissioner’s Office), though we’d appreciate the chance to help first.

California and other US state residents have the right to know what personal information we collect, use and disclose, to access, correct and delete it, and to opt out of its “sale” or “sharing” for cross-context behavioural advertising. In the last 12 months, Palm Court has collected identifiers (random player ID, IP address), internet activity (gameplay) and approximate location derived from IP address, from you and your device, for the purposes above. We don’t sell personal information for money and we don’t share it for cross-context behavioural advertising. If you want to exercise a right, email us. We don’t knowingly sell or share the personal information of anyone under 16, and we won’t treat you differently for using your rights. You may use an authorised agent; we may ask for reasonable details to verify a request.

Canada and Quebec residents can ask to see, correct or delete the personal information we hold about them, to withdraw their consent, and to ask how it is used, under Canadian federal privacy law (PIPEDA) and, in Quebec, the Act respecting the protection of personal information in the private sector (as amended by Law 25). Email palmcourt.game@gmail.com and the person responsible for privacy at Palm Court will answer. If you are not satisfied, you can complain to the Office of the Privacy Commissioner of Canada or, in Quebec, the Commission d’accès à l’information du Québec. Your personal information may be stored and processed outside Quebec and Canada (for example by our hosting and backup providers); see “Who receives data”.

We aim to respond to requests within one month (45 days for US state requests). For data held by another provider, such as the hosting or connection services above, use their privacy tools or contact them.

Security

Connections to our providers use encryption (HTTPS and encrypted WebRTC), the online backup is protected so that only your browser’s anonymous session can read or change your save, and we keep the data we collect to a minimum. No system is perfectly secure, so please don’t put personal details in your names.

Contact and changes

Questions about this policy or your data: palmcourt.game@gmail.com. If this policy changes, the new version is posted on this page with a new effective date; for significant changes we will try to give notice on the website or in the game.
