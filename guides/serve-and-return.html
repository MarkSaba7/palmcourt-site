Serve and return: toss, type, target

Every point starts with a serve. Learn one reliable first serve and one safe second serve, and you win free points. Then learn to return the other player’s serve and you stop losing them.

Written by the Palm Court team · Updated 12 October 2026 · 4 min read

The toss and the swing

A serve has two steps: toss, then swing. The toss is how you start it, and the swing is timed to the ball falling.

ControlTossSwing
MouseFlick up, or clickSwipe as the ball drops
KeyboardSpaceSpace again as the ball drops
PhoneTap the screen or lift the phoneSwing as the ball drops
CameraRaise your hand above the toss lineSwing as the ball drops
ControllerPress a shot buttonKeep holding it and let go near the top of the toss

The serve prompt on screen tells you when it is your turn and what to do. The better your timing, the closer the ball goes to where you aimed, and the less it scatters.

Where a serve must land

A serve goes diagonally across the net into the service box, which runs from the net to the service line, 6.4 metres (about 21 feet) away. The server starts on the right for the first point of a game, and the box they aim at is the one diagonally opposite. After each point they swap sides.

In the game the aimed spot sits about 35 cm off the centre line for the T, about 55 cm inside the sideline for wide, and about a metre short of the service line. You do not need to remember the numbers; you need to remember that the lines are the edge of the target, and a serve aimed at the line sometimes lands out.

Three serves

ServeSpeedSpinUse it when
FlatThe fastestLittleYou want an ace or a weak return, and have a good first-serve margin.
KickNoticeably slower (about 84% of flat)Heavy topspinIt is your second serve, or you want a ball that dips into the box and bounces up high.
SliceIn between (about 92%)SidespinYou want the ball to curve away from the receiver, especially out wide.

On a controller you pick the serve with the button: ✕ / A is flat, ○ / B a kick and □ / X a slice. The kick lands a little shorter and clears the net by a wider margin, which is why it is the safe choice. The slice also curves sideways in the air, so aim a little inside where you want it to finish. With other controls the serve is a general-purpose ball whose speed follows how hard you swing.

Speed against safety

First serves range from about 115 to 205 km/h depending on your power. The harder you swing, the more the ball scatters. A serve at full power is the highest-risk shot in the game. A good plan:

  1. First serve at about 80% effort, aimed at a target, not at the line.
  2. Second serve with spin. The game caps a second serve's power and adds extra spin, so it is slower (about 105 to 160 km/h) but much safer. Do not try to make it a first serve.
  3. Change targets. If you always serve to the T, the receiver will wait for it. Mix wide, body and T.
  4. Serve away from the best return. Some pros return far better than others; the pro guide says who.

Count your double faults. If you hit more than one a game, you are going for too much. Use the serve speed drill in Training: twelve serves, scored by speed for every one that goes in, so recklessness costs you.

Returning serve

You do not steer your player in Palm Court, so "positioning" is decided by where you send the ball and how early you swing, not where you stand. What you control on the return is timing, direction and power.

  1. Take the serve with a medium swing. A return does not have to be a winner. On a first serve, the pace is the server's; use it. A compact, medium-speed swing aimed through the middle or cross-court is enough.
  2. Aim cross-court and deep. The net is lowest in the middle, and the diagonal gives you the most room. A deep return pushes the server behind the baseline and takes away their first-strike attack.
  3. Attack the second serve. Second serves are slower and safer. Step up the swing speed a notch and aim deep to a corner. The CPU does exactly this against you.
  4. Do not rush. Swing as the ball arrives. With a phone, camera or controller release, swinging early on a fast serve pulls the ball cross-court by accident; late, and it goes down the line or out.

The best returners in the game, like Nolan Jokavic, block a first serve back deep, cross-court or through the middle, and punish the second. That is a good model: be steady on the first, bold on the second.

Practice routine

  • Five minutes of the serve speed drill, aiming at the lit box.
  • One Rookie set where you serve only to the T and only to the wide side, to feel the difference.
  • A Club set where you track your double faults.
