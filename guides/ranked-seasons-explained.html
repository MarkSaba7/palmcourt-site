Ranked play and Ranked Seasons in plain words

Ranked is the one place in Palm Court where a result is counted against other people. Here is how a rating moves, what the tiers mean and exactly what a Ranked Season pays, with nothing promised that is not switched on.

Written by the Palm Court team · Updated 26 October 2026 · 6 min read

Ranked is under Play, Ranked, Singles. It needs the game’s online service, a display name, and your online backup switched on (Settings, Gameplay). Ranked is singles only for now. You can play doubles with a CPU partner in Training (Quick Match vs CPU) or on one screen in Couch Play. A ranked match is a short set on a surface the server picks at random, and the result moves a rating that only ranked matches can change. Quick Play, parties and friend links never do.

Your rating and placement

Everyone starts on a rating of 1,000. It follows the Elo system, the same idea chess ratings use: each match is a result for two players, and a win over a stronger player pays more than a win over a weaker one. 400 rating points is about a ten to one chance of winning. The rating cannot fall below 100 or rise above 3,200, and no single match can move it by more than 70.

How far a result moves you depends on how many ranked games you have played:

Games playedSize of each change (K)Why
The first 5 (placement)56Land near your real level quickly
6 to 3032Settle in
After 3020Small, steady changes

During the five placement games you have a rating (it is used to find opponents) but no rank is shown (these are the placement games): the screens say “Unranked, placement 2/5”. After the fifth game your rank appears.

A worked example

Two players on 1,000 meet. Each has an even chance, so in a placement game the winner gains 28 points and the loser drops 28. Once both are past 30 games the same result moves 10 points each way. Now suppose you are the lower-rated player by 200 points, so the game gives you about a 24 percent chance. In the middle of the settling-in stage, an upset win pays 24 points and a loss costs only 8. That is why a win over a stronger player pays more, and why a few results against better opponents can move you quickly.

The eight tiers

A rank is a tier, and each tier except the last is split into three divisions, I (lowest) to III (highest). The tiers start at these ratings:

TierStarts at
Ball Kid0
Club Player700
Challenger900
Contender1,050
Seed1,200
Finalist1,350
Champion1,500
Legend1,700 and up, with no divisions

Because everyone begins on 1,000, a new player who wins about half their games sits in the Challenger tier. Your rank shows on the Play page, on the Ranked page, on the match-found screen (your opponent’s tier) and on the result card, which also says “Promoted to …” when you climb.

How a match is found

The queue looks for the closest rating. A ranked search begins with a strict window of 100 points, adds 25 points every five seconds up to 700, and gives up after three minutes. When a match is found you have 20 seconds to accept. If the other player does not, you return to the queue and keep your place. The same two players are not paired twice within 90 seconds unless one has already waited 45 seconds. Region and ping are not considered, so a pair on opposite sides of the world can be matched.

Leaving, dropped links and limits

  • Quitting is a loss and is recorded as an early leave. Repeat early leaves earn a queue cooldown of five minutes, 15 minutes and then an hour.
  • A dropped link gets a 30-second reconnect window first. After that, whoever is ahead on games wins; level or almost nothing played is void and moves nothing. A flaky connection can therefore cost rating when you are behind, an accepted trade-off.
  • Limits: at most three rated matches against the same player in 24 hours (the rest are friendlies) and eight rated matches an hour.

What a Ranked Season is

A Ranked Season lasts eight weeks, on a fixed UTC calendar, so every device agrees without asking a server. It is separate from the monthly Season Points track on the Play page, which any match fills (see the Daily Cup guide). The screens always say “Ranked Season” for this one, and a banner on every online page shows its name and the days left.

What counts is a finished, rated ranked match, counted once. Quick, party and friend-link matches do not, and neither does a ranked match the server turned into a friendly because of the per-pair or hourly limits. The highest tier you stand on after any counted match is your season best.

What a season pays

Rewards are paid when the season ends, on your next visit, for the highest tier you reached, and they are cumulative: a higher tier also pays every lower tier’s reward. You must have finished at least 10 ranked games that season. Paying at the end, rather than when you first touch a tier, is deliberate: a rating can be pushed up for a moment, and a title should mean the whole season.

Highest tierAdds

Everything is Fuzz and cosmetics, never a stat, and never for sale. There are twelve season themes that rotate, so each season’s items come back after 96 weeks, and 72 reward items exist in all. The Ranked page shows a rewards card with your best tier, your games out of 10 and the next reward, a toast when you reach a new tier, and last season’s result once, with a “Got it” button.

What is not live yet

Ranked is singles only for now. The game also has a design for a softer reset of ratings between seasons that is not switched on, so this guide does not describe how ratings carry over; the Ranked page tells you if that changes.

What your first ten games look like

The first five games are placement: big swings in rating, no rank shown, and opponents found by the same closest-rating rule. By the end of them you will usually be near the tier you belong in. Reaching the ten games that a season’s reward needs takes about an hour and a half of actual play (a short set is around eight minutes in the game’s own balance model), plus time in the queue. You do not need to win them: the reward depends on the highest tier you reach, and a loss still counts as a game played.

Getting better at ranked

  • Pick the control you are steadiest with, not the flashiest. A swing you can repeat is worth more than extra pace, and the serve and return guide shows where easy points are.
  • Use Quick Play to warm up: it never changes a rating.
  • Remember gear is off online by default, so your edge is timing and shot choice.
  • The ranked limits also protect you: a streak of games against the same opponent stops counting after three a day.
