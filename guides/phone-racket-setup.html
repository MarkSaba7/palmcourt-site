Phone racket setup and tips

Your phone has motion sensors that notice every swing. Palm Court turns your phone into the handle of a racket. Setup takes about a minute.

Written by the Palm Court team · Updated 12 October 2026 · 4 min read

What you need

  • A computer (or any screen) showing the game in a browser.
  • A smartphone with a browser and motion sensors. Any recent iPhone or Android phone qualifies.
  • An internet connection on both. Using the same Wi-Fi gives the best link, but is not required.

The phone racket feels the most like real tennis, and it has the least delay of the motion controls. It does need some room, so read the safety note before you start.

Hold on tight and give yourself room. Use a wrist strap or a grippy case, stay clear of people, lamps and screens, and swing only as far as you can safely reach. A phone slipping out of a sweaty hand is the one real risk of this control.

Pair the phone

  1. In the game, open Match setup and under How do you swing? choose Phone.
  2. Press Connect phone. A pairing screen shows a QR code.
  3. Point the phone's camera at the code and open the link. The racket page opens in your phone's browser.
  4. Tap Start and allow motion access. On an iPhone, the permission is called Motion & Orientation access and the browser asks once.
  5. Wait for the game to say the phone is connected.

You cannot open the racket page by itself; it needs the code from the game, because the code is what introduces your phone to your game.

Calibrate: one forehand, one backhand

The first time, the racket page asks you to swing a forehand: "a normal side-to-side forehand, like you're hitting a ball." Then it asks for a backhand: "swing across your body the other way." This teaches it which way your phone is facing in your hand, so it knows which swing is which. Your grip is saved on the phone for next time.

Two messages you may see while calibrating:

  • "That was too gentle to count." Swing harder, like you are really hitting a ball.
  • "That swing went mostly up or down." Swing from side to side, as for a groundstroke. If your backhand attempt looks like another forehand, it says so; swing across your body the other way.

Hold the phone like the handle of a racket, with the screen facing sideways, and grip firmly.

How the swing is read

  • Timing aims the shot. Swing early to pull the ball cross-court, late to send it down the line.
  • Shape sets the spin. A low-to-high swing adds topspin; high-to-low slices.
  • Speed is power. A faster swing is a harder shot, and a harder shot misses more. Rally at a comfortable speed.
  • Serving: tap the screen, or lift the phone, to toss. Then swing as the ball drops.

The game shows a Forehand or Backhand label as the ball comes, so you always know which side to swing.

Tips for a better feel

  1. Swing like a groundstroke. A full, flowing motion across the body works best. Tiny wrist flicks may not register as swings.
  2. Do not wait for the ball to arrive and then swing. Start the swing as the ball approaches, so the fastest part lands at contact.
  3. Keep the racket page open and the phone awake while you play.
  4. Use the drills first. Try target practice before a match to get a sense of your timing.

If it will not connect

SymptomTry this
Scanning does nothingMake sure the whole QR code is in the camera's view and the screen is bright.
"Waiting for phone" for a long timeKeep both screens open and scan again. Check that the phone has an internet connection (Wi-Fi or mobile data).
The game says the link stalledThe two devices could not reach each other directly. Put both on the same Wi-Fi network and try again.
No motion on iPhoneTap Start and allow Motion & Orientation access. If you denied it, close the tab, reopen the link and allow it.
Swings feel lateA connection that has to go through a relay server is slower than a direct one. Both devices on the same Wi-Fi is the fastest.
Swings are missedSwing harder and across your body. Run the calibration again from the racket page if the phone was re-gripped.

Playing with a friend

One person can use the phone while the other plays on keys, mouse or a controller, in couch play. A single game can pair only one phone at a time, so two people cannot both swing phones in the same game.

No video or personal information travels over the phone link: only the numbers that describe each swing (its speed, direction and timing) and short status messages. The behind-the-scenes guide explains the link in plain words.
