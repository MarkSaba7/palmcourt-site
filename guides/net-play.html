Net play: approach, volley, smash and lob

The net is a short, fast, risky place. Done well, it ends points quickly; done badly, it is a target. Here is how to play it, and how to beat players who come in.

Written by the Palm Court team · Updated 12 October 2026 · 4 min read

Approach: choosing the right moment

An approach shot is the groundstroke you hit as you move forward to the net. The rule is simple: approach behind a short ball, one that landed well inside the baseline. A deep ball needs a long run to get close enough to the net, and by the time you arrive, the opponent has had time to hit past you.

  • Hit it deep. A deep approach gives the opponent less time and a weaker reply.
  • Down the line from wide. If you hit the approach from out wide, go down the line. The CPU does this too: you then end up covering the shorter reply across the court.
  • Prefer to slice it if your backhand is weaker. A slice stays low and gives you time to move.
  • Then stop and split-step. Do not run through the volley. In the game the player moves for you, so your job is to hit a good approach and then be ready to swing again quickly.

The volley: one swing, four results

A volley is hit before the ball bounces, close to the net. In Palm Court a volley's type depends on how hard you swing, and good timing is rewarded: a volley on the ball is tighter and uses the pace of the incoming ball, while a mistimed one floats and strays.

VolleyHow you play itBest for
Block volleyA short, soft swing (under about 40% power)Fast balls hit at you. Use the opponent's pace and keep it short.
Punch volleyA firmer swing, with 40% power or moreDeeper, firmer volleys to finish a point.
Drop volleyA very soft swing with backspinWhen the opponent is deep behind the baseline.
SmashA hard swing at a ball above head height (about 1.7 m)Putting away a lob that did not go deep enough.

The smash is your hardest shot at the net, much faster than a volley. But it only works on balls that are high enough; if the lob is still rising or has not cleared your head, let it bounce.

Volley rules of thumb

  1. Aim away from the opponent, not at the line. The open court is a big enough target.
  2. Volley deep first, short second. A deep first volley gives the opponent a defensive ball; the second can be an angle or a drop.
  3. Use a block on a hard ball. Swinging at a fast ball makes it fly long.
  4. Move forward as you hit. The closer you are to the net, the more of the court you can reach.

The lob

A lob sends the ball high over the net player's head, deep into the back of the court. In the game, E is a lob on the keyboard, △ / Y on a controller; a mouse player uses E too. A good lob goes over the opponent's backhand shoulder or away from where they stand, and lands near the baseline.

  • Lob when the opponent is close to the net and you cannot pass them.
  • Lob after a drop shot. An opponent who ran forward for your drop shot cannot recover to chase a lob. Carlito Alcarraza plays exactly this pair.
  • A topspin lob is an attacking shot; it dips quickly. Play it when you are not under pressure.

Passing shots

When an opponent comes to the net, you have two ways through: past them or over them.

  1. Hit into the court they do not cover. If they are on your left, hit to your right.
  2. Down the line from out wide. That is the shortest way to the open court.
  3. At the feet. Against a player right on the net, a dipping topspin ball at their feet is hard to volley well.
  4. Mix it with a lob. If they hug the net, they fear the lob; if they stand back, they fear the pass.

Drop shots

A drop shot lands just over the net with backspin. Use it when your opponent is standing deep, behind the baseline, and when you are not under pressure. A drop shot played from a stretched position is simply a gift. A gentle swipe coming down gives a drop shot with the mouse (D on the keyboard; L1 + □ or LB + X on a controller).

Playing against a net-rusher

Some pros, such as Rolf Federli, come to the net at every chance. The answer is not to panic and hit harder. Play low, slice returns to their feet to make the first volley awkward, lob over their head if they get too close, and pass only when you have a real angle. Their strength is the first volley; make it hard.
