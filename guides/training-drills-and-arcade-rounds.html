Drills, Survive and Blitz: how to practise and what each one teaches

The drills and the two arcade rounds are the quickest way to improve and the cheapest place to make mistakes. This guide explains what each one measures, how the scores really work, how to read the feedback and how to put them together in ten minutes.

Written by the Palm Court team · Updated 26 October 2026 · 6 min read

Everything here is under Play, Training: Quick Match vs CPU, Drills, the Tutorial, Survive and Blitz. The courts and modes guide has the scoring and grade tables. This page is about using them well. Each drill lets you choose how you swing, with a camera check or pairing button beside the choice, so you can practise with the control you actually play with.

Drills are a ball machine and a scorer

A drill has no opponent. A machine feeds balls to alternating sides, the game watches where each of your shots lands, and a coaching line tells you what the last ball taught. Because there is no point to lose, you can try the thing you are unsure of at no cost.

The coaching line is where the value is. After a ball it says one of a few things, and each is a specific instruction:

  • “A bit early”: wait for the ball to reach your side before you swing (or, on a controller, keep holding the button).
  • “A bit late”: start sooner, as the ball bounces on your side (or let go a little sooner).
  • “Swing!”: the ball went by with no swing; the line repeats how to swing with your control.
  • “Other way”: you swung a backhand motion for a forehand, or the reverse.
  • “Out” or “Net”: a slower, smoother swing keeps the ball in; on a serve, hit it just as the ball starts to drop.

At the end the game adds a tip based on the whole run (“most misses were late”). With a camera, it also suggests moving the Timing offset if your swings always land early or late. That is the quickest way to calibrate: see the camera troubleshooting guide.

Target practice: timing is your steering wheel

Twenty balls, with glowing zones on the far court. A zone scores 3 points, 4 if your timing was clean, and anywhere else in the court scores 1. The zones come in a set order: deep cross-court, down the line, cross-court again, the short angle, down the line.

ZoneWhere it isHow to hit it
Deep cross-courtThe far corner on the diagonal, at least 8 m from the netSwing a touch early
Down the lineAlong the sideline, at least 7 m deepSwing a touch late
Short angleWide and short, between about 3.6 and 8.9 m from the netEarly, with slice

The drill is really a lesson in how timing aims a shot: early pulls cross-court, late sends it down the line. On a controller you aim with the stick instead, and the game tells you which way to push it. Aim at the middle of each zone, not its edge, because a clean-timed ball into the zone is worth a point more.

Serve speed: consistency beats a big first swing

Twelve serves at a lit box. Each serve that lands in the box scores its speed in kilometres an hour divided by ten, and one that misses scores nothing. Run the arithmetic and the strategy follows. Twelve serves in at 120 km/h score 144 points, which is already above the A grade. To reach S (170 points), you need all twelve in at an average of about 142 km/h, or nine in at about 189. Two faults from twelve cost about 24 points, more than ten extra km/h on every other serve would add. Learn a swing that goes in, then add speed in small steps: the serve and return guide explains the toss and the types.

Rally streak: your own safety margin

Ninety seconds against a steady feeder, scored by your longest run of shots in play. The feeder moves like a Pro-level player and makes few errors, so the streak is yours to lose. Every four good returns it steps up through five tiers (Warm-up, Steady, Rolling, Pressing, Match pace) and hits a little harder. The grades start at 3 shots for C, then 7, 12 and 20. The way to a long streak is the opposite of the way to win a point: aim for the middle of the court, with topspin, at a comfortable swing speed, and let the feeder’s pace carry the ball. See the groundstroke guide.

Survive: three lives and a rising CPU

Survive is a real tiebreak-style match against a CPU that sharpens with every point you win. It starts between Rookie and Club, becomes Club after 4 points won, Pro after 9 and an extra-hard Elite after 14. Losing a point never eases it. A point you lose costs a life, and a double fault counts as a lost point, so take pace off the second serve. The round ends on the third lost point, or when a three-minute cap arrives, which pays a 150-point “Still standing” bonus.

The scoring rewards runs: 100 points per point won, a streak bonus that grows from 15 on the third point in a row to a maximum of 60, 50 for an ace, 25 for a winner and 25 or 50 for a long rally. Six points in a row are worth a good deal more than six separated ones. Replays and the umpire’s voice are off in rounds, and the pause between points is a quarter shorter.

Blitz: 90 seconds against Club

The clock starts with your first serve and stops when you pause. When it runs out, the point in play is finished (for at most 12 seconds). The CPU is Club level the whole way. In a normal match the game’s balance model averages roughly eleven seconds a point, and rounds trim the pause between points, so expect something like ten points in a round. That makes time, not lives, the limit: an ace is worth 150 points in a few seconds, while a 14-shot rally is worth 150 too but takes far longer. Pick a first serve you can land, and take the early chance.

Practising with your own control

  • Camera: do the Camera check’s Timing step once, then use target practice to see whether your swings land early or late. The coaching line and the offset work together.
  • Phone: calibrate a forehand and a backhand first, then use the rally streak to find a swing size you can repeat.
  • Mouse: the drills are the easiest place to learn how fast a swipe has to be; your usual swipe is learned from your first few hits.
  • Controller: keep the aim marker on until the zones feel natural, then hide it.

A ten-minute routine

  1. One minute: a rally streak run to warm up. Aim for the middle and a streak of 7 or more.
  2. Three minutes: target practice. Read the coaching line after each ball and fix only one thing at a time.
  3. Two minutes: serve speed. Land all twelve before you add pace.
  4. Two minutes: one Blitz round, which tests it all under a clock.
  5. The rest: a Quick Match, to see if it held up against an opponent.

Drills and rounds pay a little XP and Fuzz, with daily caps, and count as a court day. They are not where progress comes from, and the first tutorial gives a one-time bonus of 100 XP and 50 Fuzz. The economy guide has the sizes. A personal best is saved for each drill and round on your device.
