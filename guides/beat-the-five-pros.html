The pros

How to beat each of the five pros

Each pro has a distinct way of playing, and the CPU plays that way. Learn what each one wants to do, and you can make them do what you want instead.

Written by the Palm Court team · Updated 12 October 2026 · 5 min read

The five pros are fictional characters (see the pros page for their looks and background). Each plays at the CPU level you pick, and each has a style: the nine tendencies in their profile (aggression, topspin, slice, drop shots, net play, serve, consistency, defence and speed) plus a few habits, such as always kicking to your backhand. The advice below comes from those styles. It is a plan, not a guarantee: the CPU level still matters, and a Rookie Federli is far easier than a Pro one.

Opponents unlock with your level: Jokavic is free; Navidal arrives at level 5, Federli at 10, Sinnari at 15 and Alcarraza at 20 (or buy them early in the Store with Fuzz).

Nolan Jokavic: the wall

Right-handed, two-handed backhand. Defence and the best return in the game. Jokavic is very consistent, very fast around the court and runs everything down. He seldom comes to the net. When he is stretched he lobs, and he returns serves better than other players at the same level.

  • Do not trade rallies. He will win a long baseline exchange almost every time. Shorten the points.
  • Serve placement beats serve power. His return takes the pace and sends it back deep. Mix T, body and wide, and take the first ball after the serve.
  • Come to the net behind a deep ball. He is a defender who rarely goes forward, so a controlled approach and a volley into the open court gets you points. Watch for his lob; a smash puts it away.
  • Change the pace. Mix a low slice with a high topspin ball so he cannot find a rhythm.

Raul Navidal: heavy topspin from the left

Left-handed, two-handed backhand. Heavy, high kicking topspin, aimed at your backhand. Navidal hits the highest, heaviest topspin of the five, runs down everything and rarely misses.

  • Take the high ball early. A ball bouncing to shoulder height on your backhand is awkward. Meet it as it rises, or step back and play it with a firm topspin swing.
  • Use the slice. He hits very little slice himself, so a low slice changes the rhythm and takes the bounce he likes away from him.
  • Move the rally off your backhand. Play cross-court to his backhand when you can and go down the line to change the direction. Remember he is left-handed: his forehand cross-court goes to a right-hander's backhand.
  • Do not wait for errors. He rarely makes them. Come in on a short ball.

Rolf Federli: slice, approach and volley

Right-handed, one-handed backhand. Slice, serve-and-volley, touch at the net. Federli attacks. He serves well, comes in behind any short ball, mixes in drop shots and slices his backhand. On some first serves he follows the serve to the net.

  • Return deep and low. A return that dips at his feet makes the first volley hard. Block it back; do not try to blast it.
  • Lob when he gets close. Defence is one of his weaker areas, and a well-placed lob over his backhand shoulder forces him to turn and run.
  • Pass with the angle. When he comes in, hit to the side he does not cover. Down the line from wide is the shortest route.
  • Win the long rallies. Defence and speed are his weakest areas, so make him hit extra balls from the baseline.

Gianni Sinnari: raw pace

Right-handed, two-handed backhand. Flat, early, hard hitting, with lots of winners and lots of errors. Sinnari stands close to the baseline, takes the ball early and goes for it. His consistency and defence are the lowest of the five.

  • Give him the ball, not the pace. He loves pace he can redirect. Play high, deep topspin and a slice, which slows the ball and brings it above his strike zone.
  • Be patient. He makes more errors than anyone, so keep the ball in play and wait.
  • Respect his serve. It is strong, so on the return block the first serve back deep instead of swinging for a winner, and hold your own with placement.
  • Do not trade winners. Hitting flat at him just gives him more of what he wants.

Carlito Alcarraza: speed and variety

Right-handed, two-handed backhand. Speed, drop shots and the lob that follows them. Alcarraza never plays the same point twice. He loops, drives, drops and comes in, runs fast and, after a drop shot, will lob you when you run forward to chase it.

  • Do not get pushed back. He is most likely to play a drop shot when you are far behind the baseline. Take balls early and firmly, and send deep balls of your own so he is the one defending.
  • Expect the lob after the drop. If you chase a drop shot, he will go over your head. Reply to the drop with a deep ball, not a soft one, or stay back and use the lob as a clue.
  • Make him find the shot. His consistency is lower than Jokavic's or Navidal's, and a player who changes the shot every time misses more. Keep the ball in play and let him make the error.
  • Take away time. Early balls hurt a fast mover more than slow rallies.

Playing as a pro

You can play as any pro you have unlocked, choosing them under Match setup. You get their look and their strengths; your gear, racket paint and celebration go with you. The Locker shows a "Your stats" panel with your pro and gear together. If you pick a pro to play as, try to play like them: Federli is the easiest to learn net play with, Navidal the best for learning topspin.
