Mouse and touchpad swipe controls

The mouse is the easiest way to start and a perfectly good way to keep playing. This guide explains the swipe in detail, because a few small habits make a big difference.

Written by the Palm Court team · Updated 12 October 2026 · 4 min read

The idea: a swipe is a swing

With the mouse as your control, you do not click to hit. You swipe: a quick flick of the mouse, a swipe on a laptop touchpad or a finger across a touch screen. The game measures the swipe's direction, its tilt and its speed, and turns them into a shot. Your player runs to the ball on their own; you swipe as the ball reaches them.

Clicking or tapping still works as a medium-speed swing, and the setting Mouse swing in Settings, Controls can be switched to Click if you prefer that. Touch screens start in tap mode.

What each part of the swipe does

Part of the swipeWhat it controlsHow
Direction left or rightWhere the ball goesSwipe left to send it to the left of the screen, right for the right. Straight up or down goes straight ahead; a diagonal is half-way.
Tilt up or downSpinSwipe upward for topspin, downward for slice. A soft swipe coming down plays a drop shot.
SpeedPowerFaster is harder. The game compares each swipe with your own usual one.
When the speed peaksTimingThe fastest moment of the swipe is the moment of contact. Peak it as the ball reaches you.

A bar at the bottom of the screen shows the ball running toward the middle. Aim to have the swipe at its fastest just as the ball gets there. Too early and the shot is mistimed; too late and the ball is behind you.

Left-handed? Set Plays to Left. A swipe to the left is a shot to the left of the screen for everyone, so swiping across your body is cross-court for either hand.

Power: the 40% rule

Power is relative to you, not to the mouse. During your first few hits the game learns your usual swipe. That usual swipe is a solid rally ball. A swipe about 40% faster than usual is flat out, which gives the fastest ball but misses more often. If you only ever swipe as hard as you can, you will make a lot of errors. Rally with your usual swipe and save the extra speed for a ball you are well set up for.

Keyboard keys

You can swing with the keyboard instead, or in addition. The arrow keys aim while you swing (hold ← or →), and Space swings. Shot keys let you choose:

  • W for topspin and S for slice, or hold ↑ / ↓ while you press Space.
  • D for a drop shot and E for a lob.
  • Hold Shift to hit harder.
  • To serve: press Space to toss, then Space again as the ball drops.
  • C switches between the TV and player cameras, Esc or P pauses, F shows the frame rate.

If your hand rests on the left of the keyboard, Keyboard layout in Settings can move aiming to WASD, and every key can be changed there.

The mouse is captured while you play

To stop a swipe from running into the edge of the screen, the game hides the cursor and captures the mouse while a match is on. Esc lets go and pauses. If your browser refuses to capture the mouse, swipes follow the cursor instead. You can turn this off with Capture the mouse in Settings, Controls.

Only a quick flick counts as a swing, and it counts once. Moving the mouse around between points, the follow-through, and bringing the mouse back are not swings.

If your swipes do not register

  1. Swipe faster. A slow drag is not a flick. Think of throwing a punch, not drawing a line.
  2. Raise Swipe sensitivity in Settings, Controls. Touchpads and gentle wrists usually need it above the default.
  3. Keep the swipe on the court. On a touch screen or touchpad, a swipe that starts near the edge may be eaten by the browser.
  4. Try Click mode if swipes are not working for you at all. It is less expressive, but it is dependable.

Habits that pay off

  • Swipe through, then stop. One clean flick per ball.
  • Start with direction, then add spin. Aim cross-court with a medium topspin swipe until it feels automatic. Cross-court is the safest direction because the net is lowest in the middle and the court is longest on the diagonal.
  • Use the Training tab. The target practice drill gives you 20 balls and glowing zones to aim at; it is the quickest way to learn what your swipes do.
  • Practise serves separately. The serve speed drill gives you twelve serves in a row.
