Accessibility and comfort: text size, contrast, motion, captions and more

Palm Court has a tab of settings for making the game easier to see, hear and follow. This page says what each one does, which ones start from your system’s settings, and how to combine them for different needs. It does not pretend the game is perfect: where something is missing, it says so.

Written by the Palm Court team · Updated 26 October 2026 · 6 min read

Open Settings from the menu or from the pause menu, and choose the Accessibility tab. Everything on it applies at once and is saved with your other settings. The tab opens with a reminder that Reduce motion and High contrast start from your system’s own preferences, so if you have already told your computer you want less motion or more contrast, the game has noticed. A few of the most useful options are also on other tabs; they are listed at the end.

Seeing the game

  • Text size offers Small, Normal, Large and Extra large. It scales the menus, the match display and the captions together, so a bigger choice is felt everywhere rather than in one corner.
  • Larger HUD text goes further during a match only: a bigger score, bigger calls and bigger prompts, on top of the text size. Use both if you play from a distance, on a television or on a small screen.
  • High contrast has three settings: Follow my system, On and Off. It strengthens borders, brightens text and draws a thick focus ring around whatever is selected, which helps a lot when you navigate with a keyboard or a controller.
  • Colour-blind-safe colours change the signals that depend on colour. Line calls and the early or late timing display switch to blue and orange, and the ball’s trail turns white, so a call does not rely on telling red from green.
  • Bounce spot (Settings, Gameplay) marks where the ball will land, which helps you judge timing. Leave it on while you learn.
  • Camera (Settings, Graphics) chooses between the view from behind your player and the TV view. C switches during a match; some people read the ball better in one than the other.

Hearing the game

  • Captions show a line of commentary on the big points in practice matches and offline matches. They have three settings: Off, Captions, and Captions plus voice, which also reads the line aloud in a second voice. Captions do not appear in online matches, arcade rounds or drills.
  • Spoken score calls are the umpire saying the score. Turning them off removes only the voice: the score is always on screen as well, and so are the words that flash when a point ends (Out, Net, Ace, Winner, Double fault). A close line call also gets a line review with the distance in centimetres.
  • Early or late bar shows after every shot how early or late your timing was, so you do not need to hear the shot to know how it went.
  • Controller rumble (Settings, Controls) gives feel in the hand: stronger on big hits, a small tick on a perfectly timed shot, a long pulse on a big serve and when you win a big point or the game.
  • Sound is made of separate levels: Master, Effects, Crowd, Music and Umpire voice. You can lower the crowd, which is often the loudest layer, without touching the rest. The performance and audio guide covers the rest.

Motion, flashes and comfort

Reduce motion removes camera shake, the lens punch on a big hit and the hit flashes, and shrinks the number of screen animations. It also turns off the camera flashes and the wave in the crowd. It follows your system’s “reduce motion” preference until you choose a setting yourself, and Reset to defaults hands it back to the system. If quick pushes or flashes bother you, switch it on before you play. Instant replays can also be turned off in Settings, Gameplay, which removes the film sequences after big points.

Controls for different bodies

The game was built so that the control is the swing and nothing else. Your player runs to the ball by themselves, so there are no movement keys to hold and no fast stick work to keep up. That makes several ways of playing practical:

  • One key at a time. On a keyboard, Space swings; holding an arrow key as you press it aims, and up or down chooses topspin or slice. The controls reference shows how to remap every key, including a one-handed arrangement.
  • Click mode. If a flick of the mouse is hard, set Mouse swing to Click (Settings, Controls), where a click is the swing. A touch screen starts in this mode. Swipe sensitivity and Capture the mouse adjust the rest.
  • Controller. Remap any match button, swap the sticks, lengthen the stick dead zone, and choose between holding a button and flicking a stick (the controller guide explains both).
  • Camera. Hand cam and Body cam need no touch at all, only a swing in front of the camera, and the Camera check lets you set how hard a swing has to be.
  • Easier opponents and shorter matches. The Rookie CPU is slow and makes many errors, and a tiebreak is a few minutes long. Pick them freely; the only difference is a little less XP and Fuzz.

The game pauses by itself if the window loses focus during an offline match, and Esc always goes back one step. Menus can be driven entirely with the keyboard or a controller, with the arrow keys or the D-pad moving between rows and left and right changing a slider. When a controller needs text, such as a name, an on-screen keyboard appears and the pad drives it.

What helps screen readers and keyboards

Settings controls are real buttons, switches and sliders with labels, and sliders announce a readable value (“80%”, not “0.8”). Messages, such as a changed key or a save confirmation, are announced as status updates. The 3D match itself is a picture, so it cannot be read by a screen reader; the score, calls and prompts are text on screen, which is what the larger HUD and captions are for. If you find something that is not announced or cannot be reached, please tell us: accessibility reports are welcome.

Pace and pressure

Only one mode in the game has a clock: the Blitz arcade round. Offline matches can be paused at any moment with Esc or P, and the camera, phone and controller setups each have a check or a test screen where you can try the control with nothing at stake. Instant replays and line reviews can be skipped with a swing or Space. If a match feels too fast, the drills under Play, Training are a calm place to practise, because nobody is waiting for you there: see the drills guide, and the first-match guide for the quickest start.

Combinations that work

If you wantTry
Easier reading from a distanceText size Large or Extra large, Larger HUD text on, High contrast On
Colour-vision differencesColour-blind-safe colours on; keep the early or late bar on
Sound off, or no soundCaptions on (or Off if you find them distracting), Spoken calls off, rumble on if you have a controller
Less motionReduce motion on, Instant replays off, Camera set to the view that feels calmer
Fewer inputsKeyboard remapped to one hand, or Mouse swing set to Click

All of these settings are saved in the file that “Export my save” creates, so you can bring them to another device: see the save and devices guide.
