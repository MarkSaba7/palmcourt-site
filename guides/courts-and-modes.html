Courts and modes

Courts, twists, Survive, Blitz and drills

Three courts change how the ball bounces. Five optional twists change the rules or the air. Two arcade rounds give you a short game. Four drills train a single skill. Here is what each one does and when to use it.

Written by the Palm Court team · Updated 27 October 2026 · 7 min read

Hard, clay and grass

The surface changes how the ball behaves on the bounce. The game models two things: how much of the ball's downward speed comes back up (bounce) and how much the court grips the ball's spin (friction).

CourtBounce (energy kept)GripUnlocks
Hard76%MediumFree from the start
Clay81%: the highestHighLevel 3
Grass70%: the lowestLowLevel 6

What that feels like

  • Hard is the middle ground: a true, medium-height bounce, a ball that keeps its pace. It suits everyone and is the default.
  • Clay bounces highest and grips the spin hardest. A topspin ball jumps up and sits up; the grip also takes speed off a ball. Rallies are longer and the defender gets more time. Heavy topspin hitters, like Raul Navidal, gain the most. A flat winner is harder to hit.
  • Grass bounces low and grips least, so the ball skids through. A slice stays very low. Pace is rewarded, and so is coming to the net; a defender has less time. Rolf Federli's slice and volley game is at its best here.

Adjusting is simple. On clay, hit higher, deeper topspin and be patient. On grass, stay low, hit earlier and take the chance to finish at the net. On hard, play your normal game. Time of day (day, golden hour at level 4, night at level 8) changes the light but not the bounce, and neither does the Venue row in Match setup (Classic, then Beach at level 7 and Indoor at level 11): a venue changes the scenery and the light, never the physics.

The World Tour calendar follows the same split with hard, clay and grass events across its season, and in the monthly seasons May is Clay Month and June is Lawn Month.

Match twists

A twist is an optional rule change that makes the same court play differently. Pick one in Match setup (Play, Training, Quick Match vs CPU): the Twist row sits under Length and is set to None until you change it. The game remembers your choice. Twists are for matches against the CPU, singles or doubles, and for some Daily Cups; online, ranked and couch matches, the World Tour, Survive, Blitz and drills never use them. A small chip under the scoreboard shows the twist in force.

TwistWhat changesHow to play it
Golden PointNo advantage. At deuce (40-40) the next point wins the game, and the chip says so.Games end sooner, so every deuce is a sudden-death point. Returners get a real chance on the server's best games.
Windy DayA steady 5 metres-per-second crosswind blows one way for the whole match; four pennants by the court and an arrow in the chip show which way.It pushes every ball sideways, you and the CPU alike. Shots allow for about half of the wind and the rest carries the ball: aim into the wind, favour the cross-court ball that the wind brings back in, and keep clear of the sideline it is blowing towards. Lobs drift the most and serves the least. The bounce spot marker shows where the ball really lands.
Big ServeServes are faster for both of you, second serves too. Aces come two to four times as often.Hold serve and the set is yours, so a return of serve is worth more than ever: stand back, block it deep and start the rally.
Rally BonusA point won in a rally of 12 shots or more counts double: 0 to 30 in one point.Patience pays. A long rally can swing a game, so a safe cross-court ball is worth more than an early gamble. Your stats still count one point for it.
Feather BallsLight balls that slow down fast and float.Power fades, so placement and depth beat pace. Rallies run long and the defender has time: use angles, drop shots and the net.

A twist match pays what the same match would without it. To keep it that way the reward counts half of the aces in Big Serve and about 70 percent of the longest rally in Feather Balls; your stats, records and achievements keep the real numbers. The Daily Cup carries a twist on five days in eight, named in its headline.

Survive

Survive and Blitz are on the Training page of the Play tab, below Quick Match vs CPU, Drills and the Tutorial. Each round is a real match against the CPU, using the real physics, serves and line calls, with a simple rules wrapper on top. A round is not a match: it does not count as a win, does not touch your match stats and earns no achievements.

  • You have three lives. A point you lose costs one life, and a double fault counts as a lost point.
  • The CPU starts between Rookie and Club and gets sharper with every point you win: Club level after 4 points, Pro after 9 and an extra-hard Elite level after 14. Losing a point never makes it easier.
  • A marathon cap of 180 seconds ends the round with a Still standing bonus of 150 points.

Blitz

Blitz gives you 90 seconds against a Club level CPU. The clock starts with your first serve and stops while you are paused. When time runs out, the point in play is played to the end (up to 12 seconds). Win as many points as you can.

How rounds score

You getPoints
Each point you win100
An ace+50
A winner+25
A rally of 8 or more shots+25 (+50 for 14 or more)
Winning streak, from the third point in a row+15 per point past the second, up to +60

Your best score in each mode is kept on your device. A round pays a little XP and Fuzz (6 XP and 2 Fuzz, plus a bit for the score and a bonus for a new best), up to a daily cap of 200 XP and 50 Fuzz, after which the round still saves your score. Matches pay in full.

Tips

  • Survive: play safe early. The first few points are the easiest of the round, so use them to find your rhythm, not to chase aces.
  • Blitz: every second counts, so do not wait for the perfect ball. A steady cross-court rally wins more points than an attempted winner.
  • An ace is worth 150 points in all (100 for the point and 50 for the ace), so take a first-serve chance when you have a good target.

Training drills

Drills have no opponent. A ball machine feeds you balls and the game scores what you do. On the Play tab, open Training, then Drills.

DrillWhat you doScores
Target practice20 balls. Hit the glowing zones: deep cross-court, down the line, the short angle.3 points for a zone (4 with clean timing), 1 for anywhere in the court
Serve speed12 serves at the lit box.Each serve that goes in scores its speed in km/h divided by 10
Rally streak90 seconds against a steady feeder that speeds up every few shots.Your longest streak of shots in play
Drop shot touch12 balls, one target: a drop shot that lands short of the service line. Swing short and soft, and let the hand dip (the controller has a drop button, the keyboard has D).3 points for the zone (4 with clean timing), 1 for anywhere in the court

Each drill ends with a grade from S down to D. For target practice, S needs 50 points, A 38, B 26 and C 14. For serve speed the grades start at 170 (S), 130, 90 and 50. For rally streak it is 20, 12, 7 and 3 shots, and for drop shot touch 36, 27, 18 and 9 points. A drill pays 10 XP and 3 Fuzz, plus extra for a grade and for a personal best, up to a daily cap of 240 XP and 60 Fuzz. The first tutorial gives a one-time bonus.

The zones tell you how to aim. Early timing pulls cross-court, late timing sends it down the line, so the drill teaches timing as much as accuracy.
