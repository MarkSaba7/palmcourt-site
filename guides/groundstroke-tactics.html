Groundstroke tactics

Most points are won from the baseline by the player who makes fewer mistakes and moves their opponent first. This guide covers the choices behind every rally shot.

Written by the Palm Court team · Updated 12 October 2026 · 4 min read

Three shots from one swing

Every groundstroke has a pace, a spin and a direction. The game names the shot from its spin: a swing that adds a lot of topspin is a topspin shot, a swing with backspin is a slice, and anything in between is a drive. After each of your shots the game shows a readout such as "Cross, topspin, 118 km/h" so you can check what you hit.

ShotWhat it doesUse it to
TopspinArcs higher over the net, dips down into the court and bounces upRally safely and deep with a high margin. Your default.
Flat driveTravels fastest and lowest, with the least margin over the netFinish a point from a short, comfortable ball.
SliceStays low and floats, then skids on landingChange the pace, keep a ball low, or reply from a stretched position.

The spin works because the physics is real enough that topspin pulls the ball down, slice holds it up, and the court surface grips the spin on the bounce. That is also why the same shot plays differently on clay, hard and grass; see hard, clay and grass.

Direction: cross-court, down the line, middle

Cross-court is the shot you should play most. Two reasons: the net is lowest in the middle (91 cm) and higher at the posts (107 cm), and the diagonal of the court is longer than a straight line, so you have more room before the ball goes long. A cross-court ball also brings your opponent across the court, away from the centre.

Down the line is shorter and the net is higher out wide, so it is riskier. Use it as a change of direction, usually after you have pulled the opponent to one side with a cross-court ball.

Through the middle is the safest ball when you are under pressure. It gives the opponent no angle to attack.

How you pick direction depends on your control. With a mouse or a controller stick, you aim directly. With a phone or a camera, timing aims it: swing a little early to pull the ball cross-court, a little late to send it down the line. The target practice drill teaches this. Its zones are labelled with tips, such as "swing a touch late" for down the line.

Depth wins rallies

The single easiest improvement is to hit deeper. A ball that lands near the baseline (the court is 11.9 m from the net to the baseline on each side) forces the opponent back, gives them less time, and keeps them from attacking. A ball that lands around the service line, in the middle of the court, sits up and is an invitation. The CPU takes that invitation, and so should you.

  • Controller: push the stick up as you let go for a deeper ball; pull it down for a shorter one.
  • Mouse: a medium-fast topspin swipe is deep and safe. A very soft swipe lands short.
  • Topspin gets depth with less risk than flat, because the dip lets the ball land in from a higher arc.

When to attack

Attack a ball that is short (it landed well inside the baseline), high (it bounces above your waist) and slow. Skip the attack if you are stretched wide, off balance, or the ball is low over the net.

  1. Step in by taking the ball early: your swing arrives sooner, giving the opponent less time.
  2. Hit to the open court. A short ball brings you in; aim at the side the opponent has left.
  3. Or approach. A deep, controlled shot down the line, followed by a move to the net, is the classic way to finish. See net play.
  4. Do not overhit. Power is relative to your usual swing, and about 40% harder is flat out, with the most errors. Use 70 to 80% of your top speed on an attack and aim to a safe part of the court.

A simple point pattern

  1. Rally cross-court with topspin until you get a short ball.
  2. Attack that short ball to the open side, or approach down the line.
  3. If you are the one pulled wide, play the ball deep and high (or a slice) to buy time to recover.

Defending

When you are stretched, the goal is to survive. Play a high, deep topspin ball through the middle or a deep slice, and the next ball will be easier. A lob (E on the keyboard, △ / Y on a controller) is the emergency exit when the opponent is at the net.

Playing the percentages against the CPU

The CPU levels differ in how well they choose shots: a Rookie hits almost anywhere, a Club player plays sensible shots about half the time, and a Pro plays the percentages most of the time and misses less. Against a Rookie you can take risks; against a Pro you cannot. The best plan against Pro is to be the more consistent player and to attack only when the ball is short.

A quick drill list

  • Rally streak (90 seconds): keep the ball in play and watch the feed speed up.
  • Target practice: ten balls to deep cross-court, ten to down the line.
  • A Rookie set where you may only hit cross-court.
