Getting started

Play your first match in two minutes

You do not need to read the whole manual. This page gets you from the first screen to a finished match, and points to the longer guides for later.

Written by the Palm Court team · Updated 12 October 2026 · 4 min read

Minute one: the first screen and the guided rally

The first time you open the game, you do not land on a menu. You get one screen called How do you want to play? with a way to swing already chosen for you and a big Play now button.

  • On a computer the suggestion is Mouse: you swipe the mouse through the ball like a racket. On a touch screen it is your finger.
  • If a game controller is connected and you have pressed a button on it, the suggestion changes to Controller and the screen says which button starts.
  • The other cards are Phone racket (needs a second device), Camera and Controller. Camera has two modes, Hand cam and Body cam: both ask for your webcam and download a small tracker the first time (about 6 MB for Body cam, about 10 MB for Hand cam).

Press Play now. About a second later the ball machine feeds your first ball. The guided rally has three steps: a forehand, a backhand and a serve. Each step moves on after two good ones, or after six tries even if they were not so good, so you cannot get stuck. If you would rather skip it, press Esc or choose Skip to the menu. You can change the way you swing at any time under Settings, Controls.

What you do and what the player does

Your player runs to the ball by themselves. Your only job is the swing. As the ball comes toward you the screen says Forehand or Backhand, and you swing as it arrives. Three things you do with the swing decide the shot:

  1. Timing (or direction, with the mouse and a controller's stick) decides where the ball goes: left, right, cross-court or down the line.
  2. Shape decides the spin. Low to high gives topspin, high to low gives a slice.
  3. Speed decides the pace. Harder shots are faster and also miss more often.

That is the whole control system. The mouse guide, phone guide, camera guide and controller guide cover the details of each way to swing.

Minute two: a Quick Match

After the rally you reach the main menu. It has tabs along the top (Play, Store, Challenges, Locker, Leaderboard, Settings). You can move between them with Q and E, or with a controller's shoulder buttons. On the Play tab, press the big Quick Match tile. It starts a match against the CPU with your last settings.

Open Match setup under the tile when you want to choose:

  • CPU level: Rookie, Club or Pro. Rookie is slow and wild, Club is steady, Pro runs everything down and hits closer to the lines. Start with Rookie, move up when you win comfortably.
  • Length: a tiebreak (first to 7 points), a short set (first to 4 games) or a full set (first to 6 games). A short set is a good default; a tiebreak is five minutes at most. The scoring guide explains all three.
  • Court: hard from the start. Clay unlocks at level 3 and grass at level 6, and they play differently (see courts and modes).
  • Opponent: a random pro, a specific one, or the club player. Pros unlock with levels; the first one, Nolan Jokavic, is free from the start.

To serve, you toss first and then swing as the ball drops. How you toss depends on the control: a flick up or a click with the mouse, Space on the keyboard, a tap on the phone, a raised hand on camera, a shot button on a controller. The serve guide goes deeper.

Three settings worth knowing on day one

  • Timing meter. A readout of how well timed each swing was. Leave it on while you learn; timing is what aims the shot, so it is the fastest way to understand why a ball went where it did.
  • Swipe sensitivity (Settings, Controls). If your mouse swipes do not register, raise it. Touchpad users usually need a higher value.
  • Instant replays. Big points get a replay, and close line calls get a line review. Both can be skipped with a swing or Space, and replays can be turned off in Settings.

What to ignore for now

The Store, Locker, challenges, World Tour and Weekly Cup are all optional. They give you reasons to keep playing once you enjoy the tennis, and you will earn XP and Fuzz (the in-game currency) just by finishing matches. The progression guide explains them when you are curious.

Your progress saves automatically in your browser on this device. Private windows forget it when they close, so play in a normal window if you want to keep your level.

Stuck on the very first swing? Nothing happening on a mouse usually means the swipe was too gentle or too slow. Make a quick, deliberate flick through the ball. On a webcam, the Camera check shows what the camera sees and fixes most problems in a minute.
