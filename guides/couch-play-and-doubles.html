Couch play and doubles

Palm Court has a mode for two people on one screen, and a doubles mode where you and a partner (a person or the CPU) take on two opponents.

Written by the Palm Court team · Updated 12 October 2026 · 4 min read

Two players, one screen

On the Play tab, choose Couch Play. The setup screen has two cards, one for each person, and each picks a way to swing. Each person can also pick a pro to play as and a left or right hand.

Two people can share one keyboard because each uses their own side of it. Keyboards can only register a limited number of keys pressed at once, so if one of you holds many keys, the other's presses can be lost; a controller each avoids that.

Why only one phone or camera

One camera follows one person, and one phone link pairs one phone. So in a couch match at most one of the two people may choose Phone or a webcam. The other person's cards for those controls are greyed out with the reason shown, and keys, mouse and controller stay open. If you use a camera, ask the other player to stay out of its picture.

When a person has a phone or a webcam, the game runs that device's usual checks first (the pairing screen or the Camera check), then starts the match. If the device fails, the setup offers Go on with keys. If it drops out in the middle of a match, the match pauses and the pause menu offers "carry on with keys" for that player, so nobody is surprised by what happens next.

What couch play does not do

A couch match is for fun, so nothing it does is recorded: no XP or Fuzz, no match history, no stats, no challenges and no tour progress. Your own control choice stays as it was when the match ends, even if a device was borrowed for the game.

Doubles

Doubles is two against two on a wider court. In Palm Court it is available in Quick Match (you and a CPU partner) and in couch play. Pick Doubles in the Match chip.

  • Quick Match doubles: you and a CPU partner against two CPU opponents.
  • Couch doubles: the two people are partners (Player 1 and Player 2, on the near end) against two CPUs. The CPU level is set on the couch screen.

The rules that change

  • The court is wider. The doubles alleys (the extra strips at the sides) are in play for rallies.
  • A serve must still land in the normal service box, the same one used in singles. The alleys are in play for rallies only.
  • Partners take turns to serve in alternating service games. The serve prompt on screen names whoever serves, and is empty when a CPU serves.
  • The scoring is the same as in singles. See tennis scoring explained.

Who hits the ball?

In doubles only one partner plays each ball. When both partners are people, the game decides: "whoever gets there more easily", with the ball in a person's own half first. Only the person who owns a ball can hit it; the other person's swing is a whiff. A person who lets a ball go loses it to their partner, so you cannot stand and watch a ball you are supposed to take. During a serve, the receiver's partner holds their position.

Doubles tactics in short

  1. Serve to the T so the receiver has to hit a ball you can reach at the net.
  2. Move together. When one partner goes to the net, the other should hold the baseline. Two players at the net, or two at the baseline, are easy to lob and pass.
  3. Look for the middle. A ball hit between the two opponents often leaves each of them thinking it is the other's.
  4. Volley early. See the net play guide.

Tips for a good couch session

  • Pick levels fairly. Give the better player a harder CPU in doubles, or play different pros to even things out.
  • If you share a keyboard, check both sides work in the pause menu before you start.
  • "Play again" keeps your choices (doubles, the same opponents) so a rematch takes one click.
