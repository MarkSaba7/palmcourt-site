Camera setup: Hand cam and Body cam

The Camera control needs no equipment, but it is only as good as the picture the camera sees. Ten minutes of setup makes the difference between a game that follows you and one that misses half your swings.

Written by the Palm Court team · Updated 12 October 2026 · 4 min read

Hand cam or Body cam?

Camera is one control with two modes. Pick Camera in the game, then choose Hand or Body.

ModeWhat it followsDownloadBest for
Hand camYour racket handAbout 10 MB, onceNothing to hold; needs good light
Body camShoulders, elbow and wrist of your racket armAbout 6 MB, onceFollowing your whole arm, wherever you stand

Both work by analysing the camera picture inside the page, on your own computer. The frames are not recorded and are not sent anywhere. The first time you pick Hand cam or Body cam, a small tracker model downloads and is then kept by your browser. The setup and lighting advice below is the same for both.

Light matters more than the camera

Most "the camera does not work" problems are lighting problems. The game measures the brightness of the picture and tells you when it is too dark or when you are backlit. You can do the same by eye:

  • Put the light in front of you, not behind. Face a window or a lamp. A bright window behind you turns you into a silhouette that no tracker can read.
  • Brighter means faster. In a dim room a webcam exposes for longer, so a fast hand smears across the picture and swings arrive late. If the picture is very dark, nothing tracks at all.
  • Avoid blown-out white. A lamp pointing straight into the lens washes the picture out. Angle it at your face or the wall.
  • A plain background helps. A busy wall or a second person in the picture gives the tracker something else to follow.

Where the browser allows it, the game asks the camera for a faster shutter and for 60 frames per second. A bright room lets the camera say yes; a dim one makes it refuse.

Setting up each mode

Hand cam

Stand about 2 metres from the screen so your head and shoulders fit the outline shown, and so your hand stays in the picture on both sides of your body. Swing across your body, as for a groundstroke. Taking your hand back first is fine; it counts as the wind-up. To serve, raise your hand above the toss line, then swing as the ball drops.

Body cam

Stand about 2 metres back so your head, shoulders and both arms are in the picture. Your legs do not need to be. Body cam reads your racket wrist against your shoulders, so where you stand in the room does not matter, and neither does walking a step to either side. Raise your hand above your head to toss. If the body tracker cannot start (for example, the download fails), the game falls back to Hand cam and says so.

The camera follows one person. If someone else wanders into the picture, the tracker may jump to them, so ask spectators to stay out of the shot.

Run the Camera check

The first time you choose the Camera control, the Camera check opens. It is worth reading, not clicking through. It shows:

  • What the camera sees, with the tracked hand or arm drawn on top.
  • A live brightness message: too dark, a bit dim, or you are backlit.
  • The camera's real frame rate and its delay.
  • Sliders for sensitivity and a timing offset. If your swings feel consistently late, move the offset so the game compensates for the camera's delay.

You can reopen it from the menu whenever the room or the camera changes.

Fixing common problems

What happensWhat to try
"The camera is blocked"Click the camera icon in the address bar and allow the camera for this page. If the browser never asked, check your system's camera privacy settings.
"The camera can't be opened"Another app or tab (a video call) is using it. Close it and try again.
No camera foundPlug one in, or switch Controls to Mouse.
The tracker could not be fetchedCheck your internet connection and reload. The mouse needs no download.
Swings are lateAdd light in front of you, step back to about 2 m, and use the timing offset in the Camera check.
Swings are missedSwing across your body with a full motion, and keep your hand or arm inside the picture for the whole swing.

Swing naturally

The camera rewards a smooth, repeatable swing. Do not flail; the tracker reads the shape and speed of a groundstroke, not a wave. Swing the way you would hit a forehand and a backhand, hold your finish for a moment, and let the player do the running. If you have a friend with a phone, the phone racket is the alternative with the least delay, and if your room is too dark for any camera, the controller and the mouse need no light at all.
