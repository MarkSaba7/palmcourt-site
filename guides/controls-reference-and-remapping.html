Controls reference: every key and button, and how to remap them

Palm Court lets you change almost every key and every match button. This page is the full map in one place, the steps for changing it, and the few rules that keep a remap from locking you out.

Written by the Palm Court team · Updated 26 October 2026 · 7 min read

The mouse, controller and couch play guides explain how each control feels. This one is for looking things up: which key does what, what you may change, and where to change it. Everything below is under Settings, Controls, and every choice is saved with your settings (and travels in a save file).

Keyboard: the two starting layouts

The keyboard has no movement keys, because your player runs to the ball. Every key is a swing, an aim or a game action. Keyboard layout picks where you start from:

How the keys combine: the key press is the moment of contact, like a click. The aim keys you are holding at that moment steer the ball left or right. Holding up with a plain swing gives topspin and holding down gives slice, the same as a swipe. A plain swing is a medium-pace ball; holding Shift pushes a drive close to full power, and the risk of an error rises with it. To serve, press the swing key to toss and press it again as the ball drops.

Keys are stored by their position on the keyboard, not by the letter on them, so the layout follows you onto an AZERTY or Dvorak keyboard, and the Settings list shows the letters your own keyboard prints.

Mouse, touchpad and touch

  • Mouse swing: Swipe (default on a computer). A quick flick is the swing: direction aims, up or down chooses topspin or slice, speed is power. Click makes a click the swing, with a flick just before to aim; touch screens start in this mode, and a tap is the swing.
  • Swipe sensitivity runs from 0.6 to 1.6 times. Raise it for a touchpad or a gentle wrist; lower it if movements you did not mean count as swings.
  • Capture the mouse hides the cursor during a match so a swipe never runs into the screen’s edge. Esc lets go and pauses.

Controller: the default buttons

With the default Buttons scheme you hold a shot button and let go as the ball arrives; the left stick aims. The names below are for a PlayStation pad with the Xbox name after the slash, and the match is by position on every other pad.

The other scheme, Stick flick, swings with a flick of the right stick (speed is power, up for topspin, down for slice) or a pull of the triggers, with the left stick aiming. Swap sticks reverses the two for left-handed players. The rest of the block: Power meter and Aim marker (both can be hidden once you no longer need them), Aim style (Zones snaps the stick to eight directions, Free keeps the exact angle), Stick dead zone (0.10 to 0.40, starting at 0.22, so a drifting stick does not aim by itself) and Controller rumble. Test the controller shows every button and the live charge and aim.

A keyboard can only report so many keys held at once, so a controller each is more reliable if you both hold several. Couch controllers always use the game’s own buttons.

How to change a key

  1. Open Settings, Controls and scroll to the Keyboard list. Actions are grouped as Shots, Aim, Game and Menus; each row shows its keys with a Change and a Reset button.
  2. Press Change on the row. A card says “Press a key”.
  3. Press the key you want. If another action already uses it, the two swap, so you never end up with one key doing two jobs. The message at the top of the list says what moved.
  4. Changed rows are highlighted. Reset sends one row back; Reset all puts the whole list back.

A new binding is a single key. A preset’s spare second key (the J swing on the WASD layout) comes back with Reset.

Keys that are never offered

  • Esc always goes back and pauses. Tab and Backspace move around the menus.
  • The system and browser keys: Windows or Command, Ctrl, Alt, the menu key, Print Screen, Caps Lock, Num Lock, Scroll Lock, Pause, and all function keys F1 to F12.
  • Shift is kept for “Hit harder”.
  • A match key may not be the camera key, but a match key may also be a menu key: E is the lob in a rally and the next tab in a menu, and that is fine because the two never happen together.

How to change controller buttons

Under the same Controls tab, Controller buttons lists eight match actions: swing, topspin, slice, lob, drop shot, hit harder, camera and pause. Press Change, then press the button you want on the pad; its name comes from the controller you have plugged in, with Xbox-style names until one shows up. The same swap rule applies. Refused on purpose: the D-pad (the menus use it), the home or guide button (your system’s), Start for anything but pause (it always pauses), and a stick click for a shot (clicking a stick moves it, which would spoil your aim). The menus keep their own buttons, so you can never remap yourself out of the game: ✕ / A picks, ○ / B goes back, and the shoulder buttons switch tabs.

If the game says no controller is found, press any button on it with the window in front: Windows only lists a pad after that.

How to change couch keys

In the same Keyboard area, open Couch play keys: two people on one keyboard. Each side lists swing, topspin, slice, lob, drop shot, hit harder and the four aim keys, with Change and Reset per row, Reset side and Reset all. The rule that makes a shared keyboard work is that a key can belong to only one of you; a key the other person uses is refused, and so are C, F, P and Esc.

Ideas worth trying

  • Playing with one hand: pick the WASD layout so aim sits under your left hand, then change Swing to a key beside it (Q works, and sharing it with the menu’s previous-tab key is allowed). Hold W or S as you swing for topspin or slice and A or D to aim. Move Lob and Drop shot to R and G if you want them too.
  • Left-handed pad: switch on Swap sticks, and set Plays to Left in Settings, Gameplay.
  • A cramped keyboard: the WASD layout puts aim under the left hand and shots under the right.
