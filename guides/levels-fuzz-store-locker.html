Behind the game

Levels, Fuzz, the Store and the Locker

Everything you unlock in Palm Court is earned by playing. There is no real-money purchase anywhere in the game: Fuzz, the in-game currency, cannot be bought.

Written by the Palm Court team · Updated 12 October 2026 · 4 min read

XP and levels

Every match, drill and arcade round earns XP, and XP raises your level, up to level 50. The first level-up costs 600 XP, and each level asks a little more than the last, so the early levels come quickly (about two matches for level 2) and the late ones take longer.

A match pays like this (for a short set; a tiebreak pays about a third of it and a full set pays 60% more):

A harder CPU multiplies the total: Club by 1.25, Pro by 1.6. Very long matches cannot mint unlimited rewards, because each bonus has a cap (for example, points count up to 150 per match).

Fuzz

Fuzz is the game's currency. You earn it alongside XP and spend it in the Store. Fuzz is never sold for money, and nothing in Palm Court takes real-money payments. The Store is a way to spend what you earn.

Pros are also for sale in the Store (1,000 Fuzz for Navidal, up to 4,000 for Alcarraza), so you can have one before you reach their level.

The Store

The Store sorts what you can buy into two groups:

  • Gear (rackets, strings, shoes, grips and dampeners) changes how you play.
  • Style (outfits, racket paint, headwear, wristbands and celebrations, plus the pros themselves) changes how you look.

The Store's top row shows what you can afford now and what you are closest to. Items unlock by level and cost Fuzz: a Club-tier racket costs around 500 Fuzz, Pro-tier items 2,000 or more, and Legend items over 20,000.

How gear works

Gear comes in five tiers: Starter, Club, Pro, Elite and Legend. Everyone starts with the neutral Starter kit. Every better item trades something off. A power racket costs you a little control; a control racket costs you a little power; spin strings take away some feel for drops and slices. So higher tiers are better, but there is no single best item, and you choose gear to suit your game.

Each piece of gear can be upgraded from +1 to +5. Each upgrade makes the item's strengths 6% stronger; its costs stay the same. In online matches gear is switched off by default, so online play is even.

The Locker

The Locker is where you equip what you own: the gear slots (racket, strings, shoes, grip, dampener) and the style slots (outfit, racket paint, headwear, wristbands, celebration and title). A panel called Your stats shows your pro and your gear together as bars around 1.0, so you can see what each choice does. If you play as a pro, they wear their own kit but your racket paint and celebration come along, and your gear goes with you on top of their strengths.

Daily and weekly goals

  • Challenges: three daily goals, with one free swap per day. They reset at midnight UTC.
  • Achievements: long-term goals with titles and rewards.
  • Weekly Cup: a themed match that changes each week, played with a neutral kit so everyone is on even terms. Bronze, Silver and Gold rewards pay once each per week.
  • Court days: any day you finish a match or drill counts. The total never goes down, and nothing resets to zero.
  • Seasons: each month has a theme, and Season Points from your play fill a six-step track that ends in a title and a racket paint.
  • Next goal: one card on the Play page shows the closest thing you can finish, usually about a match away.

The design rule here is simple: no streaks that punish a missed day, no countdown pressure, and rewards that are never taken away.

Saving and moving your progress

Progress saves in your browser automatically. If you turn on the optional online backup (on by default; see the privacy policy), a copy is kept under a random player ID so it can be restored. To move to another device, use Settings, Profile, Other devices to get a short one-time code, or sign in with an email address (optional; no password, just a code sent to you).

A sensible path

  1. Play Rookie short sets until you win comfortably, collecting levels 1 to 3.
  2. Try clay at level 3 and grass at level 6, so you meet each surface early.
  3. Spend your first Fuzz on gear that matches your style, not on the most expensive item.
  4. Move to Club, then Pro CPU. The multiplier makes it the fastest way to level.
