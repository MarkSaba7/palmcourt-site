Controller guide: buttons, aim and charge

A controller needs no camera, no phone and no room to swing. The trick is to think of every shot as a charge and a release: hold, wait, let go as the ball arrives.

Written by the Palm Court team · Updated 12 October 2026 · 4 min read

Get the game to see your controller

Plug in a PlayStation, Xbox or Switch controller, or pair one over Bluetooth, then press a button. Browsers only show a controller to a page after a button has been pressed. As soon as the game sees it, the menus work with it: the D-pad or left stick moves, ✕ (PlayStation) or A (Xbox) picks, ○ or B goes back, and the shoulder buttons switch tabs. The menu then offers to switch your swing control to Controller; you can also choose it under Settings, Controls. Any pad your browser reports in the standard layout works, including the DualSense, DualShock 4, Xbox, Switch Pro and Steam Deck.

Text entry (such as a name) uses an on-screen keyboard that you drive with the pad.

Charge, then release

The core idea: hold a shot button to charge it, and let go as the ball arrives. The moment of release is your swing. Letting go a little early pulls the ball across the court; a little late pushes it the other way, just like timing a mouse swipe or a racket swing.

  • A quick tap is a solid medium ball.
  • A full charge, about 0.8 seconds, is flat out: your fastest ball, and the one that misses most.
  • A small meter by your player shows the charge, and a marker on your opponent's court shows where the ball is going.

Your player runs to the ball. You choose the shot with the face buttons and aim with the left stick.

On a Switch controller the buttons are matched by position, so the bottom face button acts like ✕ / A, the right one like ○ / B, the left one like □ / X, and the top one like △ / Y.

Aiming with the left stick

The left stick aims where you let go. Left is the left of the screen and right is the right, whichever stroke or hand you play. Up hits deeper, down plays a shorter, sharper angle, and a centred stick plays a safe ball through the middle. The marker on the court follows the stick, and a stick that springs back as your thumb lets go keeps its aim for a moment, so you do not lose your target by releasing the stick and the button together.

Aiming close to the lines at full power misses more. A good habit: aim for the middle third of the court and use the stick only to choose a side, until your timing is dependable.

Serving

Press a shot button to toss, keep holding it, and let go near the top of the toss. The button chooses the serve:

  • ✕ / A: a flat serve, the fastest, with the least margin over the net.
  • ○ / B: a kick serve, slower, with heavy topspin so it dips in and bounces up.
  • □ / X: a slice serve, which curves away sideways.

The left stick aims the serve wide, at the body or at the T, and holding R1 / RB goes flat out for the lines. See the serve and return guide for when to use each.

Settings worth a look

Settings, Controls has a block of controller options:

  • Controller scheme: Buttons (described above), or Stick flick, where you flick the right stick to swing.
  • Swap sticks for left-handed players.
  • Power meter and Aim marker can be switched off once you no longer need them.
  • Aim style: Zones snaps the stick to left, right, up, down and the diagonals; Free uses the exact angle.
  • Stick dead zone if your stick drifts, and Controller rumble.
  • Test the controller shows every button and the live charge and aim. Use it before blaming the game.

Contact makes the controller rumble, harder on big hits, with a small tick when you released at exactly the right moment. That tick is your best feedback for timing.

Troubleshooting

  • Controller not found: press a button; browsers do not report a pad until you do. Chrome, Edge, Firefox and Safari all work.
  • A button does the wrong thing in Firefox: a PlayStation pad can arrive there in a raw layout. Palm Court remaps it and says so under Settings, Controls, but if a button is still wrong, try Chrome or Edge.

Two controllers can also be used at once for couch play.
